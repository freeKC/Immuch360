# Immuch360

**Your media. Any screen. Every perspective.**

<p align="center">🌐 <a href="https://immuch360.com/"><b>immuch360.com</b></a></p>

Flat, 360° and 3D photos and videos from your device, your NAS, your Immich server, your cameras and your drone. No account needed. Immuch360 is a free and open source app that reads your photos and videos where they are and plays each one in the viewer that fits it: flat, as a sphere you look around in, in 3D, or all around you in a headset. It runs on phones, tablets, TVs, Meta Quest headsets and computers. Its website, [immuch360.com](https://immuch360.com/), shows what it does on each screen, in 89 languages.

**New in builds 33 to 43.** [Films pick up where you stopped](#pick-up-a-film-where-you-stopped). They start sooner, and from a slow server they [play on instead of stopping](#when-a-video-waits-or-will-not-play). The timeline [zooms out to months and years](#find-a-month-or-a-year-in-a-long-timeline) on phones and computers. On an Android phone or a Meta Quest, ["Browse a folder"](#browse-a-folder-of-your-phone-or-headset) opens a folder of the device like a share, and TIFF photos show. Android TV [searches by date with the remote](#on-android-tv-and-google-tv), a Meta Quest 3 or 3S [opens Gaussian splats](#explore-gaussian-splats-in-a-meta-quest-3-or-3s) (experimental), turned with the right thumbstick and SPZ version 4 included, and a computer [makes the 3D versions of the photos of your NAS](#turn-one-photo-into-3d-a-scene-a-panorama). On a Windows laptop with two graphics chips, the app now [runs on the fast one](#when-a-video-waits-or-will-not-play), so 8K films play smoothly. Gaussian splats now [open on computers and Android phones and tablets](#explore-gaussian-splats-on-your-phone-tablet-or-computer), a Windows computer [makes splat scenes from your photos or a video](#make-a-splat-scene-from-your-photos-or-a-video-ai) by AI, your Immich server [keeps them inside photos](#keep-a-splat-scene-on-your-immich-server) that open from your timeline, and computers [play AV2 videos](#play-av2-videos-on-your-computer), all experimental. From build 42, the raw videos of Insta360, GoPro MAX and DJI Osmo 360 cameras, from your Immich library, a share or a folder, [play stabilised with the camera's gyroscope](#stabilise-your-360-videos-with-the-cameras-gyroscope-experimental) on Android phones, tablets and TVs, the splat maker of desktop build 22 [offers a Detailed quality](#make-a-splat-scene-from-your-photos-or-a-video-ai) for scenes you look at closely, and a 360° panorama [starts with 3.5 GiB of the graphics card free](#turn-one-photo-into-3d-a-scene-a-panorama), all experimental. The iPhone and iPad app is [on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/immuch360-360-3d-player/id6817655965), version 3.3.2. From build 43, the app's update notice opens Google Play or the App Store on phones and tablets; its website, [immuch360.com](https://immuch360.com/), opened on 7 November.

## Who it is for

- **You shoot 360° or 3D** and want to look around in your shots, not see them flat, doubled or stretched: [360°, VR180 and 3D](#explore-panoramas-360-vr180-and-3d).
- **You film with a 360° camera in your hand, on a bike or on a helmet** and want the raw videos of your Insta360, GoPro MAX or DJI Osmo 360 camera, kept in Immich, on a share or in a folder, to play level and steady on your phone and your TV (experimental): [stabilised with the camera's gyroscope](#stabilise-your-360-videos-with-the-cameras-gyroscope-experimental).
- **Your photos and films sit on a NAS, a computer, Plex or Immich** and you want them on the TV without copying anything: [on the TV](#watch-on-the-tv-with-the-remote), [NAS, shares and Plex](#play-the-files-of-a-nas-a-computer-a-media-server-or-plex).
- **You have a Meta Quest** and want to step inside your own photos and videos: [in the headset](#step-inside-your-photos-and-videos-with-a-meta-quest).
- **Your photos are flat** and you want them in 3D, as a scene to lean into in a headset, or inside a 360° panorama, without a 3D camera: [turn one photo into 3D, a scene, a panorama](#turn-one-photo-into-3d-a-scene-a-panorama), new, made by AI on your own computer.
- **You run Immich** and want its app, plus the immersive viewers and the big screens you sign in from your phone: [your Immich library](#keep-your-immich-library-with-more), [compared with Immich](#compared-with-immich).
- **You watch your home with Tapo cameras** and want them next to your photos, on the TV too (checked against a simulated camera): [home cameras](#watch-your-home-cameras-on-the-big-screen).
- **You fly a DJI drone** and want its live picture on your phone without DJI Fly, and in a Meta Quest or on an Android TV for the people with you (experimental): [your drone](#see-what-your-drone-sees-on-your-phone).
- **You capture places and objects in 3D, as Gaussian splats,** or would like to make them from your own photos, and want to walk through them on your phone, your computer or in a headset (experimental): [on a phone, tablet or computer](#explore-gaussian-splats-on-your-phone-tablet-or-computer), [made from your photos](#make-a-splat-scene-from-your-photos-or-a-video-ai), [kept in Immich](#keep-a-splat-scene-on-your-immich-server).
- **You have AV2 videos**, the format that follows AV1, and want to watch them on your computer (experimental): [AV2 on your computer](#play-av2-videos-on-your-computer).
- **You have several screens at home** and want them to work together: [what your screens do together](#use-cases).

There is no Immuch360 account to create, and nothing goes to us: the app talks only to your devices and to the servers you choose, and downloads an AI component or the AV2 decoder only when you ask for it.

### What your screens do together

Install Immuch360 on each screen you watch on: each one reads your sources itself, and some hand a picture, a file or a sign in to another. In each picture, the Immuch360 icon marks the devices that run the app.

#### Your NAS on every screen

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/usecases/uc-01-nas.jpg" width="720" alt="Illustration with real captures of the app: a NAS on a shelf in a living room at dusk, linked to a phone, a tablet, a TV, a headset and a laptop; the NAS feeds every screen, each running Immuch360">
</p>

**You need:** a NAS with an SMB or WebDAV share, and Immuch360 on each screen you watch on.

Add the share on each screen, and each one plays the photos and videos where they sit, nothing copied: phones, tablets, Android TV, a Meta Quest, Windows, Linux and macOS computers, and LG TVs signed in to your Immich server (SMB from webOS 22). A 360° photo turns as a sphere on the phone and the TV, and surrounds you in the headset. ## Get it on your device

Take the app from the store of your device where it is published, or from the [Releases](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases) page meanwhile. The [download page of the website](https://immuch360.com/download/) gathers the same links.

1. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.aprogsys.immuch360), or the APK of the [Releases](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases) page: `Immuch360-v<version>-arm64-v8a-release.apk` for a phone; the universal `Immuch360-v<version>-release.apk` works everywhere.
2. The app installs next to the official Immich app. The APK from GitHub does not update itself: watch the Releases page; once you have installed the app from a store, take the updates from that store. From build 43, the button of the app's update notice, in the profile menu, opens the app's page on Google Play, or on the App Store on iPhone and iPad.

### iPhone and iPad

1. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/immuch360-360-3d-player/id6817655965): version 3.3.2, published on 6 November 2026; version 3.3.3, the same app with an update notice that opens the App Store, submitted to Apple on 8 November 2026. Or build the app with Xcode on a Mac, see [Build it yourself](docs/immuch360/build.md).
2. Tap "Use without a server", or "Connect to an Immich server" to sign in with your server; both are on the welcome page from build 24.

### Android TV and Google TV

1. Until Google Play offers the app on TVs, take the universal `Immuch360-v<version>-release.apk` of the [Releases](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases) page.

3. From a computer on the same network, with adb (Android SDK Platform Tools): `adb connect <address of the TV>`, accept the prompt on the TV, then `adb install -r Immuch360-v<version>-release.apk`.
4. The app shows among the apps of the TV, with its banner. Install the next release the same way: `-r` keeps the login and the settings.

### LG TVs (webOS)

1. Version 1.0.0 is in LG's review. Until it is on the LG Content Store, it installs in Developer Mode: you need an LG TV with webOS 5.0 or later, a computer on the same network and a free LG developer account. On the TV, install Developer Mode from the LG Content Store, sign in, turn Dev Mode Status on and let the TV restart; note its IP address and the passphrase the app shows.
2. On the computer, install the webOS CLI (Node.js 18 or later): `npm install -g @webos-tools/cli`, then register the TV with `ares-setup-device` (its IP address, port 9922, user `prisoner`) and `ares-novacom --device <name> --getkey`, which asks for the passphrase.

The [webOS Dev Manager](https://github.com/webosbrew/dev-manager-desktop) of webosbrew does the same without the command line.

### Watch your photos and videos, with or without a server

Immuch360 is first a gallery and a player for ordinary photos and videos, and it needs neither an account nor a server. "Use without a server" opens the app on the photos and videos of the device, with every viewer, the 360° list and the network sources, and nothing leaves the device. With an Immich server, the timeline, the albums, the favorites, the memories and the search come from it. Videos play in a native player with sound, seeking, a buffering indicator and a choice of audio tracks, in the 360°, Spatial and computer players and, from build 29, in the flat player of the Android app, which shows subtitles too.

<img src=".github/readme/b19-library-no-server.png" width="260" alt="The Library tab without a server: the 360° entry at the top, then On this device with two 360° photos, and Network shares with a share named NAS">

1. Open the app. The welcome page shows "Your media. Any screen. Every perspective." and three choices: tap the first one, "Use without a server". A server can be connected later, from Settings or the Library tab.
2. Allow access to your photos and videos. If you refuse, the app says "The app cannot see the photos of this device", with "Allow access" and "Open the settings".
3. The Photos tab shows the timeline of the device; Search and Albums stay greyed out, as they come from the server. The Library tab has 360° at the top, then On this device (the albums of the device) and Network shares. The menu at the top right says "This device only": nothing is sent anywhere.
4. Tap a photo or a video to open it; swipe left and right to go to the next one, pinch or double tap to zoom. Swipe up on a video for its details: codec, size, frame rate, bit rate, and whether this device decodes it.
5. On Android phones, tablets and TVs, from build 29, a film with several audio languages or with subtitles has an audio button and a subtitles button in its controls: the audio language you pick is kept for the next films, and subtitles stay off until you pick them, except forced ones. The Immich app's Android player has neither.

On a computer, the folders you choose play the part of the gallery; on a TV, which has no photos of its own, the network sources do.

More: [without a server or an account](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#without-a-server-or-an-account), [the viewers and the video player](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#watch-your-photos-and-videos), [video details and decoders](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#video-details-decoders-and-why-a-video-stutters).

### Explore panoramas, 360°, VR180 and 3D

Immuch360 knows flat photos and videos, panoramas, 360° photos and videos, VR180, stereoscopic 3D, Apple spatial photos and the 360° spheres of DJI drones, and opens each one in the viewer that fits it. A 360° photo opens as a sphere you turn with a finger or by moving the phone; a 360° video plays in a spherical player with sound; VR180 files are drawn on a half sphere; stereoscopic 3D files show one eye on a flat screen and both eyes in a headset. Partial panoramas keep their crop, and the picture starts on the view the camera recorded. A file the server does not flag can be viewed as 360° with one tap.

A Quest 3 or 3S does the relief itself, for any flat photo. Everything runs on your own computer and headset: no cloud, no account, your originals never changed. New in build 32 and desktop build 13, experimental and on by default. The Immich app has nothing like it.

On a Quest 2 or a Quest Pro the button stays greyed, "Not tested on this headset yet", until you turn on "AI relief on Quest 2 and Quest Pro (untested)" there.
- **3D photos and scenes from the computer**: "View in 3D" shows a 3D photo in true 3D. "View the 3D scene" on the original, or "Explore the 3D scene" on the scene, puts the scene in your room: lean in, or glide with the left thumbstick inside the rebuilt area ("Room to move: about 0.5 m each way" for the room shown above); the layers flatten and darken at its edge, Reset, in the panel that A, X or grip shows, places the scene again in front of you, and B or Y goes back.
- **Panoramas**: a generated panorama opens all around you, facing your photo, with "Generated by AI beyond the photo's edges" and a thin outline of the original photo, which fade after a few seconds; its 3D version is in true 3D.

**On phones, tablets and TVs**, the files a computer made open as they are, and nothing is generated there. A panorama opens in the 360° viewer with the "360° AI" badge, the sentence and the outline on Android phones, tablets and Android TV; on iPhone, iPad, LG and Samsung TVs it opens as a plain 360° photo for now. A scene shows as its photo, with a small 3D SCENE mark in its corner; on Android, its details add "Open it on a Meta Quest to explore it". A 3D photo shows its two eyes side by side.

**What is real and what is not, always said.** It is the point, not the small print. The relief of a 3D photo is estimated from one photo, and what the photo hides is filled in and is not real. A scene is "Reconstructed from one photo, explore within its limits". A panorama keeps your real pixels where they were and invents the rest, about 90% of the sphere for a typical photo, and the box says how much before you start. The app says it where you look, with "Relief estimated by AI", "Generated by AI beyond the photo's edges" and the badges, and the files say it too: in our XMP, and for the scenes and the panoramas in the IPTC digital source type of an image made with AI, so that other apps can tell a generated panorama from a real 360° photo. Your originals are never changed, and nothing leaves your computer or your headset. As the box puts it: "A finished conversion does not guarantee a good result."

**What was checked**, by 29 October 2026, with test builds of this code: 3D photos by the Linux app in a container on a Linux PC, offline too; scenes by the Windows app on a laptop with an RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (ten in 2 min 39 s) and by the engine on the Linux PC; on that laptop, four panoramas in 4 min 16 s to 4 min 44 s each, their 3D versions in about 30 s, with Cancel, a killed app, another program holding the graphics card, and no network connection of the app during the tests.

Limits: 360° panoramas need a Windows or Linux computer with a suitable graphics card, and the box says why otherwise ("Not yet on Mac", "No graphics card with Vulkan found", "Integrated graphics share the computer's memory: too slow for panoramas", with "Try on integrated graphics (untested)" to try anyway); from desktop build 21, before a panorama starts, the app frees the graphics memory it holds itself, paused videos and cached pictures, and from desktop build 22 it then asks 3.5 GiB of the card free (below about 5.6 GiB the generation runs in pieces, slower, with the same image, and takes up to 8 GB of the computer's memory), and waits otherwise, saying what is free ([what each card can do](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#360-panoramas-and-the-graphics-card-memory)); inside a panorama, the edge of your photo can show as a sharp step in tone; most scenes say "Field of view guessed: shapes may be off"; a scene needs about 2 GB of free memory ("Not enough memory" otherwise); with AI versions backed up, Immich's duplicate finder pairs each photo with its scene and often suggests keeping the scene, the heavier file: keep the original. Phones and TVs make nothing, and the headset makes reliefs only; a scene comes from one photo, and from several photos or a video a Windows computer [makes a splat scene](#make-a-splat-scene-from-your-photos-or-a-video-ai).

**The components**: on the computer, Settings, "AI and 3D" lists the components of each feature with their size, Download and Remove, then "Remove all AI components" and "Move the AI components" to another drive; "AI features" off hides every AI button and downloads nothing. Each file is checked by its SHA-256. On Windows they sit in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\com.aprogsys\Immuch360 Desktop\ai-components`, which the uninstaller deletes (not a folder you moved them to); on Linux in `~/.local/share/immuch360/ai-components` on most systems, and on a Mac in `~/Library/Application Support/com.aprogsys.immuch360.desktop/ai-components`. In the Quest, "AI and 3D" is in Settings, Advanced, with the relief cache.

### Sign in a TV, a headset or a computer with your phone

**Scan. Sign in. Watch.** Typing a server address, an email and a password with a TV remote, or in a headset, takes minutes. With Immuch360 on your phone, signed in to your Immich server, a TV, a Meta Quest or a computer is signed in within seconds: the big screen shows a QR code, the phone scans it, you confirm, and the screen opens on your library. With the phone app, your password stays on the phone. The Immich app has nothing like it.

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/sign-in-with-your-phone.png" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: a TV shows a QR code on its sign-in page, and a phone held in front of it scans the same code">

1. On the big screen (Android TV and Google TV, the Meta Quest window, Windows, Linux or macOS), choose "Connect to an Immich server": the sign-in page shows "Sign in with your phone", a QR code and a 10 character code, beside the server form.
2. On the phone, signed in to your server: Settings, "Connect a TV or computer", then "Scan the code". In a Meta Quest, whose screen a phone cannot scan, choose "Enter a code instead" and type the code the headset shows.
3. The phone asks "Sign (the screen) in?" and shows a six digit number: check that the screen shows the same one, then tap Sign in. The screen opens on your timeline.

How it stays safe: the phone and the screen must be on the same home network, and the code lasts two minutes at most and works once. "Connect a TV or computer" lists the screens this phone signed in, each with Sign out, and Immich lists them in Account settings, Authorized devices, where you can revoke them too. Signing the phone out signs them out as well.

The phone side runs on Android, and on iPhone and iPad from version 3.3.2. Without Immuch360 on the phone, scan the code with the camera of the phone: a page served by the screen opens in the browser, for your email and password, encrypted and used once. LG TVs sign in with a QR code of their own, in the browser of the phone; from build 28 the scanner of the phone app recognises it and opens that page. Samsung TVs are signed in by typing for now.

### Explore Gaussian splats in a Meta Quest 3 or 3S

A Gaussian splat is a 3D capture of a place or an object, made with Polycam, Scaniverse, Postshot, Luma and other tools. In a Meta Quest 3 or 3S, Immuch360 sets it in front of you or all around you, at its size, and you walk around it or glide through it. Experimental, and on by default.

1. Put your .spz or .ply splats on an SMB or WebDAV share, or in a folder of the headset ("Browse a folder", or "Open a splat file" in the + menu of Network shares). They come after the photos and videos, with a "3D splat" badge and their number of gaussians.
2. Open one, then "Explore the splat scene". An object stands in front of you at eye height, a place opens around you. The left thumbstick glides through it, and the right thumbstick turns the view 30° about you, keeping the spot you reached ("Reverse thumbstick turn" turns it the other way). Reset, in the panel that A, X or grip shows, places it again from where you stand, and "Turn upright" turns one that comes in lying down or upside down, remembered for that file.
3. A splat shows 150,000 gaussians at most, and a larger one its 150,000 strongest ("Reduced to 150,000 of 452,451 gaussians"); "Large splats (experimental)", in Settings, Advanced, takes up to 200,000.
4. The headset reads binary .ply files and SPZ versions 1 to 4, version 4 from build 40.

Gaussian splats use an experimental API of the Meta Spatial SDK, which Meta neither supports nor warrants: any defect is Immuch360's own. On a Quest 2 or a Quest Pro the splats are listed, and "Explore the splat scene" says "Gaussian splats need a Meta Quest 3 or 3S". Files to try: the CC0 samples of the [splat viewer page of InnerScene](https://www.innerscene.com/tools/gaussian-splat-viewer).

Checked in a Quest 3 on 31 October: the files that the code of build 39 prepares from those samples open in the splat module of the Meta Spatial SDK, the one the app uses, each in view and upright. For build 40, the project's tests and CI checked the turn and SPZ version 4: the thumbstick moves recorded in a Quest 3 with build 39, replayed, turn the view 30° per push and never reset it, a turn keeps the spot you glided to, and the version 4 files of the tests are prepared for the headset byte for byte like their version 3 sources.

More: the notes of [build 36](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/v3.3.0-rc.0-36), [build 39](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/v3.3.0-rc.0-39) and [build 40](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/v3.3.0-rc.0-40).

### Explore Gaussian splats on your phone, tablet or computer

A splat made with Polycam, Scaniverse, Postshot, Luma or another tool, or one your computer made from your photos, opens on an Android phone or tablet and on Windows, Linux and macOS, and you walk through it as in a headset. Experimental, on by default. The Immich app opens no splat.

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/howto/splat-viewer.jpg" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: in a living room at night, a person seen from behind holds an Android phone, and a laptop on a low table shows the same room with a fireplace; both run Immuch360 and open that Gaussian splat from a NAS on a shelf">

Splats come after the photos and videos, with a "3D splat" badge and their number of gaussians.

### Make a splat scene from your photos or a video (AI)

You filmed a room, a garden or a statue and would like to stand in it again. On a Windows computer, Immuch360 Desktop rebuilds it from your photos or a video as a Gaussian splat, which you walk through in a Meta Quest 3 or 3S or in the splat viewer above. Everything runs on your computer: no cloud, no account. Experimental, on by default. The Immich app has nothing like it.

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/howto/splat-maker.jpg" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: photos taken all around a stone owl float in a ring and flow into a laptop running Immuch360 on Windows, which rebuilds the owl as a splat scene; a headset running Immuch360 waits on a side table to open it from a NAS">
</p>

You need a Windows computer with a graphics card of its own, with Vulkan and 4 GB of memory or more, 8 GB of memory in the computer, and 20 to 200 photos of the place or the object, or one video of it, from 10 seconds to 5 minutes.

1. Film slowly, or take a photo at every step, each one seeing most of what the previous one saw. For a place, walk along the walls looking across it, then through the middle; for an object, walk around it twice, at its height then from a little above. Even light, no zoom, nothing moving; avoid mirrors, windows against the light and shiny surfaces.
2. Select the photos or the video, in the library or a folder, then "Make a splat scene (AI)", and say what you filmed: "A place: a room, a street, a garden" or "An object, walked around". The box says which photos it leaves out and why, what the headset will get (up to 150,000 gaussians), about how long it takes on this computer, and where the file goes: next to the photos, or a folder of your choice. "Also save a .ply for other 3D apps" keeps the same gaussians as a .ply too.
3. The box offers its two components, to download once: [COLMAP](licenses/colmap/README.md) 4.2.1 (BSD 3 Clause), about 10 MB, which places the photos in 3D on the processor, and [Brush](licenses/brush/README.md) 0.3.0 (Apache 2.0), about 16 MB, which trains the splat on the graphics card. Both are built by the project's CI from their sources, signed by APROGSYS and checked by their SHA-256; then they work offline.
4. "Make the scene". The panel shows each step, taking the frames, placing the photos in 3D, training the splat, with the time left, and Cancel stops it. On a laptop with an RTX 4060, about 12 minutes for 150 photos of a room, and 8 for a one minute video of an object.
5. The scene, `<name>_splat.spz`, goes next to the photos or into your folder. Open it in the headset from your NAS or from its Download folder, where it shows whole, or in the splat viewer of a computer, a phone or a tablet, where "Save to Immich" keeps it on your server. An object is cropped around it for the headset, so that it opens in front of you; the .ply keeps the whole scene.

**Quality**, from desktop build 22: under "What did you film?", the box offers three lines, each naming the devices it is made for, with the time it would take on this computer and the size of its file ("About 13 min on this computer, a file of about 3.4 MiB"). "Headset (Meta Quest, phone, tablet, PC)" is chosen each time the box opens.

- "Headset (Meta Quest, phone, tablet, PC)": the scene of the steps above, up to 150,000 gaussians, 5 to 6 minutes of training on the laptop with an RTX 4060.
- "Detailed (PC, recent phone; headset gets a reduced copy)": the photos and frames prepared and trained at 1,920 pixels, up to a million gaussians, so that fine text, a grid or a screw come out sharp where Headset blurs them. On that laptop, 27 to 31 minutes of training, and a file of 3 MB for an object, 19 to 20 MB for a place.
- "Maximum (PC with a graphics card; headset and phone get a reduced copy)": Detailed at the full size of your photos or video, as far as this computer's memory holds it; the line says "Same as Detailed on this computer" when it does not, as for a 1080p video.

With Detailed and Maximum, `<name>_splat.spz` holds the whole scene, for computers and phones, and `<name>_splat.headset.spz` beside it, its 150,000 strongest gaussians, is the copy to open in the headset. The figures and the choice are detailed in [the user guide](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#splat-scenes-the-quality-choice).

Only what the photos saw is rebuilt: places no photo saw stay empty or blurred. When too few photos can be placed in 3D, the app says why and what to film again. Splat scenes are made on Windows with a graphics card of its own: integrated graphics, 360° photos and 360° videos are not taken yet.

Checked on the author's laptop with an RTX 4060 Laptop GPU: a room from 150 photos in 12 min 18 s, and his own one minute video of an object, filmed with a phone, 129 of 130 frames placed and the object set upright, in 7 min 47 s. The three qualities were measured on that laptop with the training program alone, on that object, a place filmed in 8K and a room of 150 photos, and the box, the files and the headset copy by the project's tests; no Detailed or Maximum scene was made in the app itself yet, and no scene of the maker was opened in a headset yet.

More: the notes of [desktop build 21](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-21) and [desktop build 22](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-22), and [the components and their sums](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/ai-components-4).

An Immich server does not take .spz or .ply files. Immuch360 saves a splat scene inside a photo of it, `<name>_splat.jpg`, which any Immich server keeps like any other photo: it shows in the timeline of all your devices, and Immuch360 opens the scene from it. Experimental, on by default, with nothing to change on the server.

### Play the files of a NAS, a computer, a media server or Plex

Your videos sit on a NAS, a computer, a media server or Plex. Immuch360 finds the servers of your network by itself, browses them and plays their photos and videos in place, in every viewer of the app, without copying anything: SMB (Samba, Windows shares), WebDAV (Nextcloud, Synology and others) and DLNA media servers (Jellyfin, Emby, minidlna, a NAS, a TV box). A phone can also share its own gallery on the Wi-Fi for a headset or any WebDAV client, and so can a Windows computer.

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/b19-add-share.png" width="260" alt="The Add a share page: Name, Server name or address, Port (optional), Share, Choose a share, Start folder (optional), User name, Password, Test the connection, and the result Connected, 2 entries in the start folder">
  <img src=".github/readme/b19-share-folder.jpg" width="260" alt="A folder of a network share as a grid of thumbnails: 360° photos with the 360° badge and a 360° video with a play mark, the Select button at the top right">
</p>

1. Open the Library tab, then Network shares, then + and "A network share (NAS, computer, media server)". From build 24, "Add a network share or a media server" on the welcome page opens Network shares directly, without a server.
2. Tap a server under "Found on the network", or fill the form by hand: type, server name or address, share or path, start folder, user name and password.
3. Tap "Test the connection", then Save. Tap the share to browse it: folders first, then the photos and videos, with the 360° badge on the files recognised as 360°.
4. For a Plex server: in Network shares, + and "A Plex Media Server", then tap your server under "Found on the network", paste a token once ("How to get it" on the page says where to find it), then "Test the connection" and Save.
5. With an Immich server connected, select files in a folder and tap "Upload to Immich" to send them to your server.

The players read the bytes they need through a bridge inside the app, with byte ranges, so seeking works and nothing is stored on the device; an SMB video is read over up to six connections in parallel for smooth playback. From build 29, films recorded as M2TS (Blu-ray, AVCHD camcorders, TV recorders) play in the Android app from SMB and WebDAV shares and from DLNA servers that list them as transport streams, and a DLNA server that lists a size of 0 no longer gives an empty video.

**Plex Media Server, without plex.tv**: the app pairs with your server directly, with a token you paste once, and reads the original files of your libraries live, so 360° stays 360°, at home and, through your port forwarding, away from home. On an LG TV, Plex is signed in with a code on plex.tv/link.

More: [network shares](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#network-shares-a-nas-a-computer-or-a-media-server), [share this phone on the network](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#share-this-phone-on-the-network), [DLNA media servers](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#dlna-media-servers), [Plex Media Server](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#plex-media-server-without-plextv).

### Pick up a film where you stopped

You come back to a film a day later and do not know where you were, nor which films you finished. Immuch360 keeps it for you: the tile of a film shows how far you watched, and the film plays on from there.

1. A video longer than 10 minutes counts as a film. Its tile shows a bar of how far you watched, and a check once you watched it to its last 2 minutes.
2. Open it again: it plays on from where you stopped, with "Resumed at 42:10" for a few seconds. No button, no popup, and swipes and the keys of a remote work as before.
3. "Play from the start" and "Mark as not watched" are in the menu of the viewer and in the details, which also say "Watched 42 %" or "Stopped at 42:10 of 01:40:00". On a TV, the remote reaches them in the details.
4. In Settings, Asset Viewer, Videos, "Remember where I stopped" turns it off, and its slider sets the length of a film, from 0 (every video) to 60 minutes. Films no longer loop; shorter videos keep looping.

It works in the viewer of your Immich library, in the player of the network shares and of the folders of the device, in the immersive view of the Meta Quest and on computers; the 360° players of phones and computers start a film from the beginning. What you watched stays in a database on the device and is never sent to your server.

Checked by the project's CI and on its build host, with build 38 and desktop build 18: the whole Dart suite passed, with the tests written for the films, from the bar on a tile to the resume in the immersive view of the Quest.

### When a video waits or will not play

A film from your server or your NAS plays from the file this device decodes, and when it has to wait, it says why and for how long.

- **The file that decodes plays.** Immich keeps the original of a video and, when it transcodes, a copy. The app plays the one this device decodes: "Playing the original: the server's copy (H.264 5760 x 2880) is above this device's decoder". When the server cannot stream that original, the app downloads it first ("Downloading the original to play it: 45 %"); when neither file decodes, it says what to change in Immich. Swipe up on a video: the "Video source" tile says which file plays and why, and its button plays the other one.
- **A slow link.** Under the spinner, "Loading: 2 Mbit/s of the 8.1 Mbit/s this video needs" and "Starts in about 37 s": the app waits for what the link carries, then plays longer stretches. "The server sends this video at 2 Mbit/s, it needs 8.1 Mbit/s: it will pause now and then" comes with the way out: in Immich, Administration, Video transcoding, a lower target resolution or a maximum bit rate.
- **A server that stopped.** "The server stopped sending this video. It plays on as soon as the server answers": the film waits for the server and plays on once it answers. The same holds for a share.
- **A laptop with two graphics chips.** From desktop build 21, the Windows app runs on the fast chip: on the author's laptop, an 8K HEVC film that showed 15 to 19 pictures a second, with black frames, on the integrated chip now shows about 30, with none. In the details of a playing film (I on the keyboard), "Video decoding" names the graphics card and the decoder ("Hardware, on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU • d3d11va"), and for a 4K or 8K film, when Power saving in the graphics settings of Windows keeps the app on the integrated chip, it adds where to choose High performance: Windows Settings, System, Display, Graphics, then restart the app. On battery, Power saving there keeps the app on the integrated chip and saves power, at the cost of 4K and 8K playback. On an integrated chip, 8K plays better than with desktop build 20, but not smoothly: about 23 pictures a second, with a few black frames.

iPhone and iPad keep their system decoders and their own waiting rule. Checked on an Android emulator against a server and a share slowed down to between 0.6 and 3 Mbit/s: films that build 38 gave up after 20 s now play, and a film whose server went silent for a minute played on 1.3 s after the server answered.

More: the notes of [build 37](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/v3.3.0-rc.0-37), [desktop build 17](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-17), [build 39](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/v3.3.0-rc.0-39), [desktop build 19](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-19) and [desktop build 21](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-21).

### Your DJI drone (experimental)

A Drone card in the Library, experimental and on by default, gathers what Immuch360 does with a DJI drone: on an Android phone, the drone's live video through its remote controller, and its photos and videos brought to Immich over USB; in a Meta Quest or on an Android TV, that live video relayed by the phone, to watch. Immuch360 is not a DJI product and is not approved by DJI, and nothing is sent to DJI.

<a id="your-dji-drones-live-video-on-your-phone-experimental"></a>
#### See what your drone sees, on your phone

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/drone-live-phone.png" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: two hands hold a drone remote controller with a phone clipped on top, whose screen shows a live aerial view of a coast, while a small drone flies over the bay at dusk">
</p>

See what your DJI drone sees on your Android phone, without DJI Fly and without any account: plug a DJI RC-N3 remote controller into the phone with its cable, and Immuch360 shows the drone's live video full screen in landscape, H.265 in 1080p, with the flight values read from the cable. The remote keeps full control of the drone. It was tried with an RC-N3, a DJI Neo 2 and a Samsung Galaxy S24+: on the ground with build 29, and in flight with test builds of build 30 on 24 and 25 October, then with build 30 on 26 October.

**Which remotes and drones**, on 26 October 2026: the live video comes through a DJI remote that holds the phone on its cable. Tried another pair? On the live screen, open Advanced, then Diagnostics, and tell us in [Issues](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) which drone and remote you used and what Diagnostics shows.

- **Tested**: a DJI Neo 2 fitted with its Digital Transceiver, which it needs to fly with any remote, and a DJI RC-N3, with a Samsung Galaxy S24+; on the ground on 23 October and in flight, indoors, from 24 to 26 October.
- **Expected to work**: the RC-N3 with the other drones DJI lists for it (Neo, Flip, Mini 4 Pro, Mini 5 Pro, Air 3, Air 3S, Lito 1, Lito X1, and the Avata 360 by its own guide), and the RC-N2, which holds the phone on the same cable, with the same drones and the Neo 2. The app shows H.265 video only, and the flight values are proven on the Neo 2 only: with another drone the picture or the values may be missing.
- **Not supported**: remotes with their own screen (DJI RC, RC 2, RC Pro 2), and so the Mavic 4 Pro, which DJI ships with one of them; DJI goggles; the RC-N1, the RC-N1C and older remotes; a drone flown from the phone over Wi-Fi, without a remote.

It only listens. No command is ever sent to the remote or the drone: the one byte the page writes each second, from 20 seconds after the remote opens, only tells whether the remote takes data, and carries no command.

On the live screen: the drone's battery and the motor time and, in flight, the height above the take off point, the vertical speed, the speed and the distance below the drone. A value goes there only once our own flight captures prove it: the height, the speeds and the distance below were proven on the captures of two indoor flights on 25 October, up to 1.8 m high and 1.5 m/s, not beyond yet; showing them there is new in build 30 and first tested with it. The height shows about 1.5 s after lift off, once the drone has left the ground or the hand, and stays hidden until the motors stop if it ever jumps in a way no aircraft can. Advanced adds the battery's voltage and temperature, the heading (its zero not checked against north) and the motors, and Diagnostics every number the link gives, the battery current included. No satellites, position or distance from home are shown. The numbers are not compared with DJI Fly yet: do not fly by them.

**Obstacle ring**, on by default: when the drone's sensors see something close, a translucent circle shows around the middle of the picture, with an arc toward the obstacle, amber turning red as it gets closer, its distance beside it and a short tick that speeds up; with nothing close, nothing shows. The circle and the direction of the arc were checked in flight, indoors, on 25 October, on a test build where the direction was an option; shown by default, it is first tested with build 30. In Advanced you set the distances ("Glow from", 3.0 m, and "Strong glow from", 1.0 m), choose a frame at the edges of the picture instead of the circle, or turn off the ring, its sound or its vibration. It shows only what the drone's sensors report: nothing in Sport mode or with obstacle avoidance off, and no glow does not mean the way is clear. Information only: Immuch360 never steers or stops the drone.

1. The Drone card is in the Library, and "DJI live video" on its page: "DJI live video (experimental)", in Settings, Advanced, is on by default.
2. Turn on the drone and the remote, close DJI Fly, then open "DJI live video" and read the safety notice.
3. Plug the remote into the phone with its cable. If Android asks which app should open it, choose Immuch360 without "Always", which would take the remote away from DJI Fly; if it asks for a USB mode, choose charging only. Plugging in the remote then opens this screen by itself ("Open this screen when the remote is plugged in", on by default in Advanced).
4. Remote, Drone and Picture light up in turn, and the picture comes full screen within a few seconds. Three buttons (leave, "Send to a headset or a TV" and Advanced) and the values sit in the black bands beside the picture. The notes, such as "Live", fade after about five seconds; a tap anywhere brings them back, and nothing on the picture acts on the drone. An error that asks you to act stays.
5. Advanced has "Save a capture for a bug report", which writes the link's capture to `Download/Immuch360/dji-link`, "Play a saved capture", to play one again without the remote, and Diagnostics, with every number the link gives.

The drone's photos and videos cannot be reached through the remote, which does not open the drone's storage: copy them from the drone itself over USB or from its card, as [below](#bring-a-drones-or-a-camera-cards-photos-and-videos-to-immich). Keep the saved captures to yourself, as the page says: they hold the drone's video and the serial numbers of the remote and the aircraft, and may hold its position; a report in [Issues](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) with what Diagnostics shows helps, above all with other DJI remotes.

The drone's picture reached a Quest 3 once, on 24 October, over a home Wi-Fi; the headset window and the flight view with the values and the obstacle ring are first tested with build 30. Should the circle hide the middle of the picture in the headset, "Frame at the edges" in its menu keeps the middle clear. On a TCL Android TV, the pairing and the picture were tried on 25 October, with stutter and a green cast on the earlier relay; on 26 October, with build 30, after the switch to the steady stream, the TV kept showing the picture through a flight where the drone sent 18 Mbit/s, with 7 freezes, the longest 1.7 s; how the picture looked was not recorded. From build 31 the safety notice on the TV keeps the focus of the remote (checked by widget tests); in build 30 the view under it could take the focus, so the notice could be neither read nor accepted, and the only way out was Back, which leaves the page. Flying while you watch the headset or the TV needs an observer next to you who keeps the drone in sight (EU open category); without one, watch the drone.

1. On the phone, on the live screen, tap "Send to a headset or a TV" and turn it on.
2. **Meta Quest**: open Immuch360, Library, Drone, and pick the phone; type the code the phone shows, once for that phone. The picture opens in the window; "Open the flight view" makes it larger, and B or Y brings the window back.
3. **Android TV and Google TV**: open Immuch360, Library, Drone. Under "Let this TV watch", the TV shows a code and its QR code: on the phone, in the same panel, tap "Add a TV" and scan it, or type it. Then select the phone on the TV and press OK: the picture comes full screen; OK shows or hides the numbers, and Menu opens the options.
4. The phone and the screen must be on the same local network. Away from home, turn on the phone's hotspot, in the 5 GHz band if it offers it, and join it from the headset or the TV.
5. For a bug report, the headset's menu and the TV's Options have Diagnostics and "Save a reception record", which keeps what that screen received.

**Relay quality**, new in build 30, in Advanced on the phone: in flight the drone sends about 18 Mbit/s, and on 25 October a home Wi-Fi path from the phone through the router to the TV carried that full stream only a third of the flight time; the rest of the time the TV's picture stuttered, broke up and took a green cast. With Automatic, the default, a screen whose Wi-Fi cannot keep up gets a steady picture that the phone encodes again for it, up to 1080p at 30 pictures a second and at the rate its Wi-Fi carries, made to stay clean, with less detail, instead of breaking up; it goes back to the drone's own picture at the next landing or take off, once its Wi-Fi has carried the steady picture for 30 s without a stall; on 26 October that TV's Wi-Fi never did, and the TV stayed on the steady picture, at 15 pictures a second for 44 of its 59 seconds. Full stream always sends the drone's picture as it comes, Steady stream always the phone's. The steady picture first ran on a phone on 26 October, with build 30: a Galaxy S24+, encoding it in hardware HEVC at a constant bit rate. A TV on an Ethernet cable, or a screen joined to the phone's own hotspot, leaves one Wi-Fi hop instead of two.

Not in this build: the drone on iPhone and iPad (not started), the relay to LG and Samsung TVs, and the proof of the values above 1.8 m and 1.5 m/s, then the satellites and the distance from home, which wait for an outdoor flight.

#### Bring a drone's or a camera card's photos and videos to Immich

Import the photos and videos of a DJI drone over USB, straight to your Immich server: plug the drone or a card reader into an Android phone, pick the new files and send them, with the videos placed on the map from the drone's flight data and the drone's 360° spheres shown as 360°. The Immich app backs up only what is already in the phone's gallery, and the Immich server cannot read the place of a DJI Neo 2 video yet. This page is experimental, on Android phones and tablets only, and was checked on an Android emulator with test files; a test with a real DJI Neo 2 is on the roadmap.

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/dji-drone-import.png" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: a drone on a terrace table, plugged into a phone by a cable, and its photos and videos going in an arc from the drone to the gallery of the phone">
</p>

1. Open the Drone card in the Library, then "Photos and videos from the drone". Its setting, "DJI drones (experimental)" in Settings, Advanced, is on by default; turn it off there to hide that page.
2. Plug the drone (a Neo 2 can stay off) or a card reader into the phone with a USB-C data cable; a DJI remote without a screen (RC-N1, RC-N2, RC-N3) holds no photos. On the DJI drone page, tap "Choose the drone or the card", choose it in the side menu of the picker, open its DCIM folder or keep the whole drive, then "Use this folder" and Allow.
3. The page lists the photos and videos by day, without the .LRF, .SRT, .THM and .m4a side files. Tap "Select all new", then "Upload to Immich"; the files sent before carry a cloud mark and are skipped. "Keep on the device" copies the files to the phone's gallery instead, in the album "Immuch360 import".
4. For a video to land on the map, turn on Video Subtitles in DJI Fly (video mode, Settings, Camera) before you fly: the drone then writes a .SRT file next to each video, the app reads the place from it and sets it on your server after the upload, when Immich found none.

The 360° spheres of DJI drones (Mini, Air, Mavic and others, recognised from their camera) show as 360° in Immuch360 even without 360° tags. Immich needs those tags: the upload option "Tag drone spheres as 360° for Immich", off by default, sends the photo unchanged with a small .xmp file beside it, so that Immich shows it as a sphere too. The Neo 2 has no sphere or panorama mode: its photos and videos are flat. When the phone recorded the sound of a Neo 2 clip, that sound is in a separate .m4a file: the page marks the clip, and Immich receives it without sound (download it with DJI Fly to keep the sound). Any camera card with a DCIM folder works the same way in a card reader. Nothing is sent to DJI.

More: [DJI drones over USB](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#dji-drones-photos-and-videos-over-usb-experimental), [DJI link diagnostics](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#dji-link-diagnostics-experimental).

### Open the raw files of 360° cameras

The raw .insp photos and .insv videos of Insta360 cameras (one or two lenses, in one track, two tracks or two files), the .360 files of the GoPro MAX and the .osv files of the DJI Osmo 360 are stitched by the app as they play, with the calibration written in the file, with nothing to install anywhere.

1. Put the raw files where the app reads them: an SMB or WebDAV share, your Immich server (it takes .insp and .insv only), or the folders of a computer. The gallery of an Android phone does not list raw files, so a raw file copied to the phone shows neither in its gallery nor in the app.
2. Keep the two files of a recording made of two files together: the app looks for the other one next to the first.
3. Open the file and tap 360°. A photo shows "Raw 360° file, stitched by the app" with the source of its calibration; a video is stitched as it plays. On Android phones, tablets and TVs, an Insta360, GoPro MAX or DJI Osmo 360 video also plays [stabilised with the camera's gyroscope](#stabilise-your-360-videos-with-the-cameras-gyroscope-experimental) (experimental).

Linux and macOS play raw videos flat, and dual fisheye .dng files show flat for now.

More: [raw 360° camera files](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#raw-360-camera-files-without-the-cameras-app).

### Stabilise your 360° videos with the camera's gyroscope (experimental)

A 360° video filmed in the hand, on a bike or on a helmet shakes, and its horizon leans. The cameras record their own motion in the raw file without stabilising its pixels. From build 42, the 360° player of Android phones, tablets and TVs reads that motion, recorded by the camera's gyroscope, and turns each frame back: the horizon stays level, the shake goes and the view follows the road when you turn. Experimental, on by default. The Immich app has nothing like it.

1. Put the raw videos where the app reads them: your Immich server takes the .insv videos of an Insta360 X3, X4 or X5 as they are, and a network share or a folder of the device also holds the GoPro MAX (.360) and DJI Osmo 360 (.osv) videos, which an Immich server does not take. Keep both files of a recording made of two files together, as in [Open the raw files of 360° cameras](#open-the-raw-files-of-360-cameras).
2. Open the video from your timeline, a share or ["Browse a folder"](#browse-a-folder-of-your-phone-or-headset) and tap 360°: it starts at once, and "Stabilized with the camera's gyroscope" shows for a few seconds.
3. The stabilisation button, in the top bar of the player next to the audio, 360° and 3D buttons, turns it off to see the video as it was filmed, and on again. "Stabilize with the camera's gyroscope (experimental)", in Settings, Asset Viewer, Videos, follows the button and keeps your choice for the next videos.

A GoPro MAX video was checked on one file: the horizon and the shake are corrected, except while the camera points near straight up or down, where part of the shake stays. A DJI Osmo 360 video goes from the raw picture to the stabilised one over its opening 5 seconds, since the camera's record of those seconds can lean by up to 20°. A GoPro MAX 2 video plays as before, without the button, and so does a file the app cannot read at any position: a file of a DLNA or Plex server that does not tell its size, a server that sends a whole file when asked for a part of it, a device that sends each file from its start. A video without a motion track plays as before, without the button, and the view can start turned to one side, the horizon level. iPhone, iPad, the Meta Quest and the computers do not stabilise yet.

Measured on six Insta360 files: the gyroscope matches the motion seen in the picture within 0.1 to 0.3° per frame, against a shake of 0.65 to 1.0° per frame on motorbike rides. Checked by the project's tests and CI on the motion tracks of sample files of Insta360 X3, X4 and X5, GoPro MAX (one file) and DJI Osmo 360 cameras, read from the Immich library and from a share. No run of the stabilised picture on a phone or a TV is recorded yet.

More: the notes of [build 42](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/v3.3.0-rc.0-42), and [issue 14](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues/14), where Aoaodacat asked for it.

### Turn a photo with one key

Rotate a photo 90° with one press, without opening an editor: a button, a key of the keyboard or a colour key of the TV remote, on every platform. The Immich app turns a photo in its editor; Immuch360 does it with one key, and for your own photos on an Immich server 2.6 or later it goes through that same server editor, so the web app and your other devices show them turned. Any other photo is turned on this device only. 360°, 180° and 3D photos never turn, so the immersive views stay level.

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/rotate-one-key.png" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: a phone shows a photo turning a quarter turn clockwise, above a row of round buttons with the rotate button lit">
</p>

1. **Phones, tablets and the Meta Quest window**: open the photo and tap Rotate in the bottom row, before Edit, to turn it clockwise; a long press turns it the other way. With a keyboard, R and Shift+R.
2. **Android TV and Google TV**: the yellow key of the remote turns the photo clockwise and the blue key counterclockwise, wherever the focus is in the viewer. On a remote without colour keys, press Down: the bar under the photo shows with Rotate selected (one Right away when the photo has a text button), and each OK turns the photo.
3. **LG TVs (webOS)**: yellow and blue as on Android TV, R and Shift+R on a keyboard, or the turn button of the photo's controls. Samsung TVs turn a photo with one key too.
4. **Windows, Linux and macOS**: R turns clockwise, Shift+R counterclockwise, or the rotate button in the top bar, before full screen.
5. **Photos of a network share, Plex or a camera**: the rotate button first in the top bar, R and Shift+R, or the yellow and blue keys.

Where the turn is saved: a photo of your own account on Immich 2.6 or later is turned through the server's photo editor, which keeps the original file and makes a turned picture (a thin bar shows while it works; four turns, or one turn back, return to the original). The photos of the device, of a share, of a partner or a shared album, and every photo on an older server are turned on this device only, for that photo, and the file is never changed; the app says so once for an Immich photo. In the Android, iOS and computer apps, a turn kept on the device shows in the viewer, not yet in the thumbnails, the slideshow and the memories. Videos, the locked folder and every 360°, 180° or 3D photo have no Rotate.

### Use your computer as the media player

On a Windows computer, Immuch360 Desktop reads the folders you choose with no server at all, plays flat and 360° videos in a player of its own, browses the same shares and servers, and can share the computer with a headset at home. Linux and macOS have it as previews, with 360° videos played flat.

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/desktop-b4-video.jpg" width="440" alt="The video player of Immuch360 Desktop on Windows: a video of a street playing in the window, with the pause button, the time bar, Share and Upload to Immich">
</p>

1. Install the preview, or unzip it and start `immuch360.exe`, see [Install](#windows). On the welcome page, "Use without a server" opens the choice of your folders, and "Connect to an Immich server" the login page, with its QR code for your phone.
2. A photo, a video or a folder opened with the program, or dropped on its window, opens at once, a 360° one in its 360° view; several files open together on one page. Photos and videos get it in "Open with"; it never makes itself their default program.
3. In a viewer, the keyboard drives it: Space plays and pauses, J and L go back and forward, F is full screen, and R and Shift+R turn a photo.

More: [on a Windows computer](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#on-a-windows-computer-immuch360-desktop-preview), [platform support](docs/immuch360/platform-support.md).

### Play AV2 videos on your computer

AV2 is the video format that follows AV1, finalised by the Alliance for Open Media in 2026, and no phone, TV or graphics card we know of decodes it yet. Immuch360 Desktop reads AV2 files with software decoding: the processor of your computer decodes them with dav2d by VideoLAN, as fast as your computer allows. Immich does not play them. Experimental, on by default, on Windows and Linux; on macOS, checked by the project's CI only, no Mac tried yet.

<p align="center">
  <img src=".github/readme/howto/av2-on-computer.jpg" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: at night, a person seen from behind watches a film of a lighthouse on a laptop running Immuch360, its files on a NAS on a shelf; below, the film's tile with its AV2 badge and the details line Video decoding: AV2, decoded by the processor">
</p>

1. Put your `.ivf` or `.obu` AV2 files in a folder of your library, or open the share that holds them, or open one with Immuch360 Desktop from your file manager. Their tiles carry an AV2 badge.
2. Open one. Once, "Download and play" fetches the AV2 decoder (about 1 MB, 1.7 MB on a Mac; the Windows one signed by APROGSYS) and checks it by its SHA-256; it then works offline, and your AV2 videos get their thumbnails.
3. "Video decoding", in the details of the video (I on the keyboard), shows how fast this computer decodes it. On the author's laptop, with an Intel Core i9-13980HX, 8 bit videos up to 1080p play smoothly, 10 bit 1080p just keeps up, and 4K does not keep up, in 8 bit as in 10 bit: such a video pauses to catch up. A video this computer decodes too slowly says so before it plays, with "Play anyway".

To turn it off: Settings, Asset Viewer, "AV2 videos (experimental)"; "Remove the AV2 decoder" is there too. Decoding uses every core of the processor and warms the computer. The phones, the TV and the Quest do not play AV2 yet, and the Immich server does not take `.ivf` and `.obu` files.

More: the notes of [desktop build 21](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-21), and [the AV2 decoder and its licences](licenses/av2/README.md), from the prerelease [av2-decoder-1](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/av2-decoder-1).

## Privacy

Nothing goes to the developer: no account, no analytics, no crash reporting service, no advertising. The app talks only to the servers you choose (your Immich server, your shares, your Plex server, your cameras) and, without a server, to nothing at all. Passwords and tokens stay in the secure storage of the device; LG and Samsung TVs have none for the app, so there they stay in the app's own storage, not encrypted. The camera is used on the device only, by the Spatial 2.5D player and to scan the code of a screen you sign in; its images are never stored and never sent anywhere. A screen signed in from your phone gets a session of its own, sent encrypted on your home network, never your password; an LG TV is signed in by a page in the phone's browser, which sends your email and password to the TV unencrypted on your home network, and the TV keeps only the session. The captures and reception records of the drone pages stay on the device unless you share them: they hold the drone's video and may hold its position, and a capture the serial numbers of the remote and the aircraft. The drone's picture and values go from the phone only to the headsets and TVs you pair, on your local network, not encrypted, as with a phone shared on the Wi-Fi. Nothing is sent to DJI, nor to TP-Link. The full policy is in [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md), and the details per source in the [user guide](docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#privacy). What you watched of your films is kept in a database on the device and never sent to your server.

**The AI features** work on your devices only. The photo, its relief, its scene and its panorama are computed on your computer or in your headset and sent nowhere. The app connects only to download a component you asked for, from the releases of this project on GitHub or, failing that, from the model's official page when it has one, checks each file by its SHA-256, and then works offline. ONNX Runtime, which runs the models on a computer, sends telemetry to Microsoft in its official builds; Immuch360 turns it off. The new files go next to your originals, never over them, and reach your Immich server only if you turn on "Also back up AI versions" ("Select all" in the backup albums turns it on too).

## Roadmap

What is not done yet, the most likely first. Nothing here is a promise, and feedback on the [issue tracker](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) helps decide what comes first.

- **The stores**: Google Play with build 43 (in Google's review since 8 November, in place of build 42; the TV listing in review), the Meta Horizon Store with build 42 (in Meta's review since 7 November), then build 43, the App Store with version 3.3.3 (submitted to Apple on 8 November), the LG Content Store (version 1.0.0 in LG's review since 24 October, then version 1.0.1), and later the Samsung store.
- **AI creator**: 360° panoramas on a Mac; splat scenes made on Linux, with integrated graphics and from 360° photos and videos; the headset copy of the Detailed and Maximum splat scenes measured in a headset; the AI labels in the iOS, LG and Samsung apps; panoramas tried on a Linux PC with a graphics card, and on AMD and Intel cards.
- **Drones**: an outdoor flight to prove the height and the speeds above 1.8 m and 1.5 m/s and the obstacle distances against a tape, then to show the satellites and the distance from home; the steady picture of the relay checked in a headset; the relay to LG and Samsung TVs; the drone on iPhone and iPad, not started; a test of the import page with a real DJI Neo 2, then the sound recorded by the phone merged into its clip. Nothing that sends a command to a drone is planned before those tests.
- **TVs**: build 29 on a real Android TV, its video controls and the sign in with your phone included; the shares, Plex and the live view of the LG app on a real LG TV, and webOS 26; Samsung test build 0.3.0 on a real Samsung TV, then the sign in with a phone, the shares, Plex and the cameras on Samsung TVs; then the 360° videos on the sphere of LG TVs, the sound of the cameras and their recordings (not before December 2026).
- **Cameras**: the Tapo cameras checked with a real camera.
- **Computers**: the test of each function by hand on a Windows PC, sign in with your phone included; Spatial 2.5D with the webcam on Windows, in development for November 2026; then the 360° video renderer on Linux and macOS, a test on a real Linux PC; then automatic updates and a signed macOS app; AV2 in MKV, WebM and MP4 files, which an Immich server takes.
- **Phones and tablets**: a time bar in the iOS 360° video player, previous and next in the 360° players of phones, audio track and subtitle menus in the iOS flat player; the splat viewer on iPhone and iPad.
- **Immersive media**: dual fisheye .dng, the stabilisation of the raw videos on iPhone and iPad, the Quest and the computers, and of GoPro MAX 2 files, spatial videos in 3D on the Quest if its decoders allow it, previous and next between spatial photos in the headset.
- **Sources**: swiping between the files of a share folder, Digest authentication for WebDAV, a QR code to bring the Plex token from the computer, IPv6 for Plex, H.265 Tapo recordings, channels on the TV home screen.
- **Upstream**: small pull requests to Immich for the parts the maintainers want, starting with the 360° photo viewer.

The full list, with the device checks still pending for each feature, is in [Release status](docs/immuch360/release-status.md#roadmap).
