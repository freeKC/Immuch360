# Immuch360

**Your media. Any screen. Every perspective.**

Immuch360 is a free and open source app to find, browse, play and explore your photos and videos: the ordinary ones, and the panoramic, 360°, 3D and immersive ones that other apps show flat, doubled or stretched. It reads them where they are, on the device itself, on a NAS or a computer, on a Plex server or on an Immich server, and plays them on phones, tablets, TVs, Meta Quest headsets and computers. No account, no cloud, no server required.

- **What does it do?** It shows your photos and videos as a gallery and plays them in the viewer that fits each file: flat, as a sphere you look around in, in 3D, in a headset.
- **Which media?** Flat photos and videos, panoramas, 360° photos and videos, VR180, stereoscopic 3D, Apple spatial photos, the 360° spheres of DJI drones, and the raw files of 360° cameras (Insta360, GoPro MAX, DJI Osmo 360), stitched by the app.
- **On which devices?** Android phones and tablets, iPhone and iPad, Android TV and Google TV, LG TVs with webOS and Samsung TVs with Tizen (a separate TV app, in test builds), Meta Quest 2, Pro, 3 and 3S, Windows (preview), and Linux and macOS as early previews.
- **Do I need a server or an account?** No. Immuch360 works on its own with the media of your device. An Immich server, a NAS, a media server or a Plex server are options you add when you want them.
- **How do I sign in a TV, a headset or a computer?** With your phone: scan the QR code its sign-in page shows, or type its code, confirm, and it opens on your Immich library in seconds, with nothing to type with a remote.

## What you can do

Each usage below says what you get, then how to do it in a few steps. The full guide, with every option, message and limit, is in the [user guide](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md).

<a id="watch-your-photos-and-videos"></a>
### Watch your photos and videos

Immuch360 is first a gallery and a player for ordinary photos and videos. The timeline, the albums, the favorites, the memories and the search work with an Immich server; the device's own photos and videos, and the folders of a computer, show without any server. Videos play in a native player with sound, seeking, a buffering indicator and a choice of audio tracks, in the 360°, Spatial and computer players and, from build 29, in the flat player of the Android app, which shows subtitles too. On a TV the remote drives everything; on a computer the mouse and the keyboard do.

1. Open the Photos tab: the timeline of your server, or of the device without a server.
2. Tap a photo or a video to open it; swipe left and right to go to the next one, pinch or double tap to zoom.
3. Swipe up on a video for its details: codec, size, frame rate, bit rate, and whether this device decodes it.
4. On Android phones and TVs, from build 29, a film with several audio languages or with subtitles has an audio button and a subtitles button in its controls: the audio language you pick is kept for the next films, and subtitles stay off until you pick them, except forced ones. The Immich app's Android player has neither.

More: [the viewers and the video player](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#watch-your-photos-and-videos), [video details and decoders](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#video-details-decoders-and-why-a-video-stutters).

<a id="turn-a-photo-with-one-key"></a>
### Turn a photo with one key

Rotate a photo 90° with one press, without opening an editor: a button, a key of the keyboard or a colour key of the TV remote, on every platform. The Immich app turns a photo in its editor; Immuch360 does it with one key, and for your own photos on an Immich server 2.6 or later it goes through that same server editor, so the web app and your other devices show them turned. Any other photo is turned on this device only. 360°, 180° and 3D photos never turn, so the immersive views stay level.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/rotate-one-key.png" width="720" alt="Immuch360 Rotate 90°">
</p>

1. **Phones, tablets and the Meta Quest window**: open the photo and tap Rotate in the bottom row, before Edit, to turn it clockwise; a long press turns it the other way. With a keyboard, R and Shift+R.
2. **Android TV and Google TV**: the yellow key of the remote turns the photo clockwise and the blue key anticlockwise, wherever the focus is in the viewer. On a remote without colour keys, press Down: the bar under the photo shows with Rotate selected (one Right away when the photo has a text button), and each OK turns the photo.
3. **LG TVs (webOS)**: yellow and blue as on Android TV, R and Shift+R on a keyboard, or the turn button of the photo's controls.
4. **Windows, Linux and macOS**: R turns clockwise, Shift+R anticlockwise, or the rotate button in the top bar, before full screen.
5. **Photos of a network share, Plex or a camera**: the rotate button first in the top bar, R and Shift+R, or the yellow and blue keys.

Where the turn is saved: a photo of your own account on Immich 2.6 or later is turned through the server's photo editor, which keeps the original file and makes a turned picture (a thin bar shows while it works; four turns, or one turn back, return to the original). The photos of the device, of a share, of a partner or a shared album, and every photo on an older server are turned on this device only, for that photo, and the file is never changed; the app says so once for an Immich photo. In the Android, iOS and computer apps, a turn kept on the device shows in the viewer, not yet in the thumbnails, the slideshow and the memories. Videos, the locked folder and every 360°, 180° or 3D photo have no Rotate.

<a id="explore-panoramas-360-vr180-and-3d"></a>
### Explore panoramas, 360°, VR180 and 3D

A 360° photo opens as a sphere you turn with a finger or by moving the phone; a 360° video plays in a spherical player with sound; VR180 files are drawn on a half sphere; stereoscopic 3D files show one eye on a flat screen and both eyes in a headset. Partial panoramas keep their crop, and the picture starts on the view the camera recorded. A file the server does not flag can be viewed as 360° with one tap.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/b19-sphere.jpg" width="260" alt="A 360° photo of a lake shore in the sphere viewer: the close button at the top left, the 360°, 3D layout and gyroscope buttons at the top right">
  <img src="../.github/readme/b19-video.png" width="420" alt="A 360° video of a coastal road playing in the 360° player of a phone held sideways: close and the file name at the top left, 360° and 3D at the top right, previous, skip back, play, skip forward and next in the middle, the time bar at the bottom">
</p>

1. Open the photo or video. 360° files carry a 360° badge on their thumbnail, and the Library tab has a 360° list that gathers them, with filters by period, source, kind and camera.
2. Tap 360° in the top bar. Drag to look around, pinch to zoom, tap the gyroscope button to look around by turning the phone.
3. The 3D button cycles "Mono (not 3D)", "3D, top and bottom" and "3D, side by side"; the 360°/180° button switches between the full sphere and the half sphere of VR180.
4. For a file that shows flat although it is a 360° one: ⋮ at the top right, then "View as 360°".

On phones and tablets, a stereoscopic video can also gain depth on the flat screen: the Spatial 2.5D player uses the front camera to follow your head and shows the view in between the two eyes (experimental). Apple spatial photos taken with an iPhone are recognised and shown in 3D in a Meta Quest.

More: [360° photos and videos](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#360-photos-and-videos-as-a-sphere), [3D and VR180](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#3d-and-vr180-photos-and-videos), [Spatial 2.5D](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#depth-on-a-flat-screen-spatial-25d-experimental), [Apple spatial photos and videos](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#apple-spatial-photos-and-videos), [the 360° list](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#find-your-360-shots-the-360-list).

<a id="use-your-own-files-without-an-account"></a>
### Use your own files, without an account

Immuch360 needs neither an account nor a server. From build 24 the app opens on a welcome page, where "Use without a server" comes first: it opens the app on the photos and videos of the device, with every viewer, the 360° list and the network sources. Nothing leaves the device. A server can be connected later, from Settings or the Library tab, and the server features then appear.

<img src="../.github/readme/b19-library-no-server.png" width="260" alt="The Library tab without a server: the 360° entry at the top, then On this device with two 360° photos, and Network shares with a share named NAS">

1. Open the app. The welcome page shows "Your media. Any screen. Every perspective." and three choices: tap the first one, "Use without a server". Up to build 23, the login page came first, with "Use without a server" under the form.
2. Allow access to your photos and videos. If you refuse, the app says "The app cannot see the photos of this device", with "Allow access" and "Open the settings".
3. The Photos tab shows the timeline of the device. Search and Albums stay greyed out, as they come from the server. The Library tab has 360° at the top, then On this device (the albums of the device) and Network shares.
4. The menu at the top right says "This device only": the app shows the photos and videos of this device, nothing is sent anywhere.

On a computer, the folders you choose play the part of the gallery; on a TV, which has no photos of its own, the network sources do.

More: [without a server or an account](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#without-a-server-or-an-account).

<a id="reach-the-media-of-a-nas-a-computer-or-a-media-server"></a>
### Reach the media of a NAS, a computer or a media server

Your videos sit on a NAS, a computer or a media server. Immuch360 finds the servers of your network by itself, browses them and plays their photos and videos in place, in every viewer of the app, without copying anything: SMB (Samba, Windows shares), WebDAV (Nextcloud, Synology and others) and DLNA media servers (Jellyfin, Emby, minidlna, a NAS, a TV box). A phone can also share its own gallery on the Wi-Fi for a headset or any WebDAV client, and so can a Windows computer.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/b19-add-share.png" width="260" alt="The Add a share page: Name, Server name or address, Port (optional), Share, Choose a share, Start folder (optional), User name, Password, Test the connection, and the result Connected, 2 entries in the start folder">
  <img src="../.github/readme/b19-share-folder.jpg" width="260" alt="A folder of a network share as a grid of thumbnails: 360° photos with the 360° badge and a 360° video with a play mark, the Select button at the top right">
</p>

1. Open the Library tab, then Network shares, then + and "A network share (NAS, computer, media server)". From build 24, "Add a network share or a media server" on the welcome page opens Network shares directly, without a server.
2. Tap a server under "Found on the network", or fill the form by hand: type, server name or address, share or path, start folder, user name and password.
3. Tap "Test the connection", then Save. Tap the share to browse it: folders first, then the photos and videos, with the 360° badge on the files recognised as 360°.
4. With an Immich server connected, select files in a folder and tap "Upload to Immich" to send them to your server.

The players read the bytes they need through a bridge inside the app, with byte ranges, so seeking works and nothing is stored on the device; an SMB video is read over up to six connections in parallel for smooth playback. From build 29, films recorded as M2TS (Blu-ray, AVCHD camcorders, TV recorders) play in the Android app from SMB and WebDAV shares and from DLNA servers that list them as transport streams, and a DLNA server that lists a size of 0 no longer gives an empty video.

More: [network shares](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#network-shares-a-nas-a-computer-or-a-media-server), [share this phone on the network](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#share-this-phone-on-the-network), [DLNA media servers](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#dlna-media-servers).

<a id="find-your-immich-library"></a>
### Find your Immich library

With an [Immich](https://github.com/immich-app/immich) server, Immuch360 is the Immich mobile app, with the same server and the same account as the web app: backup, timeline, albums, search, people, memories, sharing, partners, all synced with your server. It installs next to the official app. On top of it: the 360° badge and list, every immersive viewer, the choice between the original file and the server's transcoded stream for each video, the technical details of a video, and the files of a share or of the device sent to your server by hand.

1. On the welcome page, tap "Connect to an Immich server", then log in with your server address, your email and your password, as in the Immich app; Back returns to the welcome page. Up to build 23 the login page opens directly. Your library, albums and backup settings are those of your account.
2. In Settings, Asset Viewer, Videos, "Video source" picks the original when this device decodes it, always the original, or always the transcoded stream.
3. To show a 360° photo to someone who does not have the app, share it with an Immich shared link: the Immich web app shows it as a sphere in their browser.

The current build, build 29, is based on Immich `main` of 10 October 2026 (version 3.3.0-rc.0), like build 28, which was tested against a server built from that same Immich `main`; build 27 was also tested against an Immich 3.2.4 server. Please report problems in [Issues](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues), not to the Immich project.

More: [everything else is Immich](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#everything-else-is-immich), [compared with the Immich app](../docs/immuch360/compatibility.md#compared-with-the-immich-app).

<a id="sign-in-a-tv-a-headset-or-a-computer-with-your-phone"></a>
### Sign in a TV, a headset or a computer with your phone

Typing a server address, an email and a password with a TV remote, or in a headset, takes minutes. With Immuch360 on your phone, signed in to your Immich server, a TV, a Meta Quest or a computer is signed in within seconds: the big screen shows a QR code, the phone scans it, you confirm, and the screen opens on your library. With the phone app, your password stays on the phone. The Immich app has nothing like it.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/sign-in-with-your-phone.png" width="720" alt="A TV shows a QR code on its sign-in page, and a phone held in front of it scans the same code">
</p>

1. On the big screen (Android TV and Google TV, the Meta Quest window, Windows, Linux or macOS), choose "Connect to an Immich server": the sign-in page shows "Sign in with your phone", a QR code and a 10 character code, beside the server form.
2. On the phone, signed in to your server: Settings, "Connect a TV or computer", then "Scan the code". In a Meta Quest, whose screen a phone cannot scan, choose "Enter a code instead" and type the code the headset shows.
3. The phone asks "Sign (the screen) in?" and shows a six digit number: check that the screen shows the same one, then tap Sign in. The screen opens on your timeline.

How it stays safe: the phone and the screen must be on the same home network, and the code lasts two minutes at most and works once. The phone asks your server for a new session for that screen, named after it ("Immuch360 on Living room TV"), and sends it to the screen encrypted, so your password never leaves the phone. "Connect a TV or computer" lists the screens this phone signed in, each with Sign out, and Immich lists them in Account settings, Authorized devices, where you can revoke them too. Signing the phone out signs them out as well.

The phone side runs on Android now, and on iPhone and iPad from the next iOS release. <a id="cameras-plex-and-raw-camera-files"></a>
### Cameras, Plex and raw camera files

- **Tapo cameras**: a camera is added next to the network shares. The app shows it live (Android, Android TV, the Quest, Windows and, video only, LG TVs from webOS 0.2.0; iPhone and iPad from the next iOS release) and lists the recordings of its memory card day by day on every platform; a clip is fetched from the camera, plays like any video and can be sent to Immich. The app talks to the camera on your network only, never to the servers of TP-Link.
- **Plex Media Server, without plex.tv**: the app pairs with your server directly, with a token you paste once, and reads the original files of your libraries live, so 360° stays 360°, at home and, through your port forwarding, away from home.
- **Raw 360° camera files**: the raw .insp photos and .insv videos of Insta360 cameras (one or two lenses, in one track, two tracks or two files), the .360 files of the GoPro MAX and the .osv files of the DJI Osmo 360 are stitched by the app as they play, with the calibration written in the file, with nothing to install anywhere. On Android, play them from a share, a computer or your server, since the gallery of the phone does not list raw files; the Immich server takes .insp and .insv only.

More: [Tapo cameras](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#tapo-cameras-live-view-and-the-recordings-of-the-memory-card), [Plex Media Server](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#plex-media-server-without-plextv), [raw 360° camera files](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#raw-360-camera-files-without-the-cameras-app).

<a id="your-dji-drones-photos-and-videos-on-your-immich-server"></a>
### Your DJI drone's photos and videos on your Immich server

Import the photos and videos of a DJI drone over USB, straight to your Immich server: plug the drone or a card reader into an Android phone, pick the new files and send them, with the videos placed on the map from the drone's flight data and the drone's 360° spheres shown as 360°. The Immich app backs up only what is already in the phone's gallery, and the Immich server cannot read the place of a DJI Neo 2 video yet. This page is experimental, on Android phones and tablets only, and has not been tried with a real drone yet.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/dji-drone-import.png" width="720" alt="Immuch360, a DJI drone and Immich">
</p>

1. A "DJI drone" tile is in Library, Network shares: "DJI drones (experimental)" is on by default in Settings, Advanced. Turn it off there to hide the tile.
2. Plug the drone (a Neo 2 can stay off) or a card reader into the phone with a USB-C data cable; a DJI remote without a screen (RC-N1, RC-N2, RC-N3) holds no photos. On the DJI drone page, tap "Choose the drone or the card", choose it in the side menu of the picker, open its DCIM folder or keep the whole drive, then "Use this folder" and Allow.
3. The page lists the photos and videos by day, without the .LRF, .SRT, .THM and .m4a side files. Tap "Select all new", then "Upload to Immich"; the files sent before carry a cloud mark and are skipped. "Keep on the device" copies the files to the phone's gallery instead, in the album "Immuch360 import".
4. For a video to land on the map, turn on Video Subtitles in DJI Fly (video mode, Settings, Camera) before you fly: the drone then writes a .SRT file next to each video, the app reads the place from it and sets it on your server after the upload, when Immich found none.

The 360° spheres of DJI drones (Mini, Air, Mavic and others, recognised from their camera) show as 360° in Immuch360 even without 360° tags. Immich needs those tags: the upload option "Tag drone spheres as 360° for Immich", off by default, sends the photo unchanged with a small .xmp file beside it, so that Immich shows it as a sphere too. The Neo 2 has no sphere or panorama mode: its photos and videos are flat. When the phone recorded the sound of a Neo 2 clip, that sound is in a separate .m4a file: the page marks the clip, and Immich receives it without sound (download it with DJI Fly to keep the sound). Any camera card with a DCIM folder works the same way in a card reader. Nothing is sent to DJI.

<a id="your-dji-drones-live-video-on-your-phone-experimental"></a>
### Your DJI drone's live video on your phone (experimental)

See what your DJI drone sees on your Android phone, without DJI Fly and without any account: plug a DJI RC-N3 remote controller into the phone with its cable, and Immuch360 shows the drone's live video, H.265 in 1080p, with the battery of the remote and of the drone, the signal, the gimbal and the flight data, read from the cable. The remote keeps full control of the drone. It was tried on the ground with an RC-N3, a DJI Neo 2 and a Samsung Galaxy S24+; other remotes and drones have not been tried. The flight and battery numbers are not compared with DJI Fly yet: do not fly by them.

It only listens. No command is ever sent to the remote or the drone: the one byte the page writes each second, from 20 seconds after the remote opens, only tells whether the remote takes data, and carries no command.

1. Settings, Advanced, "DJI link diagnostics (experimental)" is on by default (never on a TV); the "DJI link" tile is in Library, Network shares.
2. Turn on the drone and the remote, close DJI Fly, then open the DJI link tile and read the safety notice.
3. Tap "Start the capture", then plug in the remote with its cable. If Android asks which app should open it, choose Immuch360 without "Always", which would take the remote away from DJI Fly; if it asks for a USB mode, choose charging only.
4. The state reads "Accessory open", then "Remote frames arrive", and the video follows within a few seconds. The button on the picture shows it full screen, and the "Flight and battery" section gives the numbers of the remote and the drone.
5. "Export the capture" writes three files to `Download/Immuch360/dji-link`; "Play a capture" plays an export again on the page, without the remote, to see how well a device decodes the drone's video.

The drone's photos and videos cannot be reached through the remote, which does not open the drone's storage: copy them from the drone itself over USB or from its card, as in [the section above](#your-dji-drones-photos-and-videos-on-your-immich-server). On a Meta Quest the page is there too, but the drone's live video does not reach the headset yet. Keep the exported files to yourself, as the page says, since they hold the serial number of the remote; a report in [Issues](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) with the states and counters the page shows helps, above all with other DJI remotes.

<a id="advanced-features"></a>
## Advanced features

What the app can read and play, for the reader who wants the whole extent. Each line is detailed in the [user guide](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md) and the [compatibility page](../docs/immuch360/compatibility.md).

- **Formats**: flat photos and videos (what the device decodes: MP4, MOV, MKV and more); equirectangular 360° photos and videos; partial panoramas (GPano crop); VR180; stereoscopic 3D, top and bottom or side by side, read from the file (the st3d box) or guessed from the frame; Apple spatial photos (HEIC stereo pairs) and spatial videos (MV-HEVC, one eye); raw Insta360 .insp and .insv, GoPro .360, DJI .osv; 10 bit HLG and PQ sources tone mapped on the sphere.
- **Projection and metadata**: GPano and spherical metadata read from the file, with range requests on a server or a share; the initial view of the camera; the spheres of DJI drones recognised from their camera, with an optional .xmp sidecar so that Immich flags them too; "View as 360°" remembered on the device; VR180 recognised from the file or its name; the place of a drone video read from its .SRT flight log; metadata kept intact because the original file is played, never a converted copy; a quarter turn saved through the Immich editor or kept on the device, never on an immersive file.
- **Native playback**: Media3 on Android and the TV, SceneKit and AVFoundation on iOS, the Meta Spatial SDK in the headset, libmpv on Windows; the original or the transcoded stream chosen from what the device decodes; a list of the device's video decoders in Settings, Advanced; the audio track choice, subtitles in the Android player and a buffering indicator.
- **Raw files stitched on the device**: a Flutter fragment shader for photos; a GPU effect in the player for single track videos; two hardware decoders at once and a GPU compositor for videos with one lens per track or per file (OpenGL ES 3 on Android and the Quest, Metal on iOS, and on Windows, from desktop build 5, two libmpv players whose frames the app pairs on the graphics chip); the camera's own calibration (Mei, V6 and equidistant models) and accelerometer record; fallbacks to one lens, the transcoded stream, the camera's LRV copy or the unstitched video when the device cannot run two decoders.
- **Sources**: the device's gallery; the folders of a computer; a drone or a camera card over USB, through the system folder picker (Android, experimental); a DJI RC-N3 remote controller over its cable for the drone's live video (Android, experimental); an Immich server; SMB 2 and 3, WebDAV, DLNA/UPnP; Plex over HTTPS checked against the server's own certificate; Tapo cameras over the local network (HTTPS and RTSP); a phone or a computer shared on the Wi-Fi as a WebDAV server; servers found by Bonjour/mDNS, a local network scan, SSDP, GDM and the TP-Link discovery, and followed when they change address.
- **On demand reading**: a local bridge serves the players with byte ranges and a per session token; large blocks, read ahead and up to six SMB connections in parallel; thumbnails from a frame of each video; nothing copied to the device.
- **Hardware**: hardware decoders used wherever they exist, with their measured limits (the Quest 3 H.264 ceiling, two 2048x2048 lenses at most for software decoders); on Windows the 360° view drawn by the graphics chip from each decoded frame, the frame size measured on each computer, the integrated or dedicated chip detected.
- **Platform specifics**: the gyroscope and the Spatial 2.5D player on phones; the immersive view, controllers and hands on the Quest; the remote layout, the keyboard dialog and the TV banner on Android TV; on the computer, mouse and keyboard, full screen, a single window, files and folders opened with the program or dropped on its window, read like the files of your folders, HEIC, AVIF, TIFF and camera raw photos of the folders when Windows has a decoder for them, and the Windows data protection for passwords and tokens.
- **Privacy by construction**: no account, no cloud, no analytics; passwords and tokens in the device's secure storage (on LG and Samsung TVs, which have none for the app, in the app's own storage, not encrypted); a screen signed in from a phone gets a session of its own, sent encrypted on the home network, never the password (an LG TV gets the password from a page in the phone's browser, unencrypted on the home network, and keeps only the session); the camera of the Spatial player and of the code scanner used on the device only; shares, Plex and cameras reached on your network only.

<a id="install"></a>
## Install

### Android phones and tablets

1. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.aprogsys.immuch360), or the APK of the [Releases](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases) page: `Immuch360-v<version>-arm64-v8a-release.apk` for a phone; the universal `Immuch360-v<version>-release.apk` works everywhere.
2. The app installs next to the official Immich app. The APK from GitHub does not update itself: watch the Releases page; once you have installed the app from a store, take the updates from that store.

### iPhone and iPad

1. The App Store version is waiting for Apple's review. Until it is published, build the app with Xcode on a Mac, see [Build it yourself](../docs/immuch360/build.md).
2. Tap "Use without a server", or "Connect to an Immich server" to sign in with your server; both are on the welcome page from build 24.

<a id="android-tv-and-google-tv"></a>
### Android TV and Google TV

1. Until Google Play offers the app on TVs, take the universal `Immuch360-v<version>-release.apk` of the [Releases](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases) page.
2. On the TV, turn on the developer options and USB debugging (on a Google TV: Settings, System, About, select "Android TV OS build" seven times, then Settings, System, Developer options; the names vary between TVs).
3. From a computer on the same network, with adb (Android SDK Platform Tools): `adb connect <address of the TV>`, accept the prompt on the TV, then `adb install -r Immuch360-v<version>-release.apk`.
4. The app shows among the apps of the TV, with its banner. Install the next release the same way: `-r` keeps the login and the settings.

<a id="lg-tvs-webos"></a>
### LG TVs (webOS)

1. You need an LG TV with webOS 4.0 or later, a computer on the same network and a free LG developer account. On the TV, install Developer Mode from the LG Content Store, sign in, turn Dev Mode Status on and let the TV restart; note its IP address and the passphrase the app shows.
2. On the computer, install the webOS CLI (Node.js 18 or later): `npm install -g @webos-tools/cli`, then register the TV with `ares-setup-device` (its IP address, port 9922, user `prisoner`) and `ares-novacom --device <name> --getkey`, which asks for the passphrase.
3. Download `Immuch360-webOS-0.2.0.ipk` from the [webOS pre-release](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/webos-v0.2.0), check it against `SHA256SUMS.txt`, then `ares-install --device <name> Immuch360-webOS-0.2.0.ipk`. The [webOS Dev Manager](https://github.com/webosbrew/dev-manager-desktop) of webosbrew does the same without the command line.
4. A Developer Mode session lasts 1000 hours and the EXTEND button of the Developer Mode app renews it; when it expires, the TV removes the apps installed this way. The details are in [webos/README.md](../webos/README.md#install-on-a-tv-in-developer-mode).

<a id="samsung-tvs-tizen"></a>
### Samsung TVs (Tizen)

1. You need a Samsung TV with Tizen 5.5 or later (2020 models and later), a computer on the same network and a Samsung account. On the TV: Apps, App Settings, type 12345, turn Developer mode on, enter the IP address of the computer, then restart the TV.
2. Download `Immuch360-Tizen-0.1.0.wgt` from the [Tizen pre-release](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/tizen-v0.1.0) and check it against `SHA256SUMS.txt`. A Samsung TV installs only an app signed for it, so the package must be signed again with a Samsung certificate that lists the DUID of your TV: [Apps2Samsung](https://github.com/Apps2Samsung/Apps2Samsung), a community tool, reads the DUID, signs the package with your Samsung account and installs it; Tizen Studio with the Samsung Certificate Extension does the same by hand.
3. Open the app and sign in by typing your server address, then your email and password or an API key. About this TV shows what the TV supports, as text and as a QR code: that text, posted in [Issues](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) with what does not work, helps the most.

<a id="meta-quest"></a>
### Meta Quest

1. From the [Horizon Store](https://www.meta.com/experiences/immuch360/1384208401440714/): the store version, updated as Meta reviews each submission.
2. Or sideload the `-quest-release.apk` file of a [release](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases) in developer mode with adb or SideQuest: `adb install -r Immuch360-v<version>-quest-release.apk`, then open the Library of the headset, "Unknown sources".
3. The store version and the GitHub APK are signed with different keys: to switch from one to the other, uninstall the app first, which loses the login, the settings and the list of network shares.

<a id="windows"></a>
### Windows

1. Download `Immuch360-Desktop-3.3.0-rc.0-desktop.9-windows-x64-setup.exe` from the [desktop pre-release](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-9) (with `SHA256SUMS.txt` to check it) and open it. The installer and the ZIP are signed; Windows shows APROGSYS as the publisher. While the publisher is new to SmartScreen, it may still ask once: "More info", then "Run anyway".
2. Choose "Install for me only" (no administrator rights) or "Install for all users", then the language of the installer, the one of Windows already selected.
3. Start Immuch360 Desktop from the Start menu. Photos and videos get it in "Open with" (right click a file, Open with); it never makes itself their default program. The first start of a new build can take up to a minute while Windows scans the files.
4. Windows 10 or 11, 64 bit. To update, open the installer of the next build: your sign in, folders and settings stay. The ZIP of the same release is the same program as a folder, to unzip anywhere and start `immuch360.exe` from it. On a computer where desktop builds 4 to 8 stopped at the start on "duplicate column name: group_date", build 9 opens that database and keeps your data.

### Linux

On Ubuntu 22.04 or later and Debian 12 or later: `sudo apt install ./Immuch360-Desktop-3.3.0-rc.0-desktop.9-linux-amd64.deb`; on Fedora 40 or later: `sudo dnf install ./Immuch360-Desktop-3.3.0-rc.0-desktop.9-linux-x86_64.rpm`, both from the [desktop pre-release](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-9). Then start Immuch360 Desktop from your applications. A preview: the folders of the computer, photos and 360° photos as a sphere, and flat videos through the system's libmpv, which the package installs (360°, 3D and raw videos play flat, with a message); SMB shares work from Ubuntu 22.04 and Debian 12 on. On Fedora, camera videos need the FFmpeg of RPM Fusion. Without GNOME Keyring or KWallet, the passwords are kept for the session only, and the settings of the app say so.

### macOS

Open `Immuch360-Desktop-3.3.0-rc.0-desktop.9-macos.dmg` from the [desktop pre-release](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-9) and drag the app to Applications (Apple silicon and Intel, macOS 12 or later). It is not signed nor notarized yet: Control click it and choose Open on macOS 14 and earlier, or choose "Open Anyway" in System Settings, Privacy & Security on macOS 15 and later. Nobody working on the project has a Mac: the app was only run on the project's CI Mac, where it opens files and folders and plays flat videos with its own libmpv; its network access has not been tried. Reports are welcome.

<a id="privacy"></a>
## Privacy

Nothing goes to the developer: no account, no analytics, no crash reporting service, no advertising. The app talks only to the servers you choose (your Immich server, your shares, your Plex server, your cameras) and, without a server, to nothing at all. Passwords and tokens stay in the secure storage of the device; LG and Samsung TVs have none for the app, so there they stay in the app's own storage, not encrypted. The camera is used on the device only, by the Spatial 2.5D player and to scan the code of a screen you sign in; its images are never stored and never sent anywhere. A screen signed in from your phone gets a session of its own, sent encrypted on your home network, never your password; an LG TV is signed in by a page in the phone's browser, which sends your email and password to the TV unencrypted on your home network, and the TV keeps only the session. The full policy is in [PRIVACY.md](../PRIVACY.md), and the details per source in the [user guide](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#privacy).

<a id="the-project"></a>
## The project

Immuch360 is an unofficial fork of the [Immich](https://github.com/immich-app/immich) mobile app, under the [GNU AGPL v3](../LICENSE), not affiliated with, nor endorsed by, the Immich team or FUTO. The fork exists because 360° viewing on mobile has been requested in Immich since January 2024 and is not in the official app yet; it ships it now, gathers real device feedback, and will offer back to Immich, in small pull requests, whatever the maintainers want. Everything the official app does is kept; the branches, the build chain and the CI are described in [Build it yourself](../docs/immuch360/build.md).

The 360° photo viewer is based on the upstream pull request [immich-app/immich#31169](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/pull/31169) by dmitry-brazhenko, itself built on the prototype by bencefr in [#30192](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/pull/30192); thanks to both. The remote control fixes of the search filters and the album sorting on TVs started from the work of mlc70 in [mlc70/Immuch-Android-TV#1](https://github.com/mlc70/Immuch-Android-TV/pull/1), and the action rail and the filter sheets on 80 percent of the screen height come from mlc70's [mlc70/Immuch-Android-TV#3](https://github.com/mlc70/Immuch-Android-TV/pull/3), with their authorship kept; thanks to mlc70. The LG webOS app, also packaged for Samsung TVs, is built on [immich-tv-webos](https://github.com/Seeky91/immich-tv-webos) by Seeky91, under the MIT licence, kept in its folder; thanks to Seeky91. The Tapo cameras were written from what [pytapo](https://github.com/JurajNyiri/pytapo), [Home Assistant Tapo Control](https://github.com/JurajNyiri/HomeAssistant-Tapo-Control) and [python-kasa](https://github.com/python-kasa/python-kasa) document. The Meta Quest view relies on the Meta Spatial SDK, which is not open source, so it stays in this fork. The SMB shares use libsmb2, under the LGPL 2.1 or later: from build 27 and desktop build 7, every build carries its notice, its licence texts and its source archive, `Immuch360-libsmb2-sources.tar.gz`, which is also attached to each release. The Windows and macOS builds carry libmpv (the library of [mpv](https://mpv.io), with [FFmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org)) built by the project, under the LGPL, with its licences and sources, listed on the licence page of the app (Licenses, at the bottom of the profile menu); the details and the trademarks of the platforms named on this page are in [License and trademarks](../docs/immuch360/platform-support.md#license-and-trademarks).

The phone captures of this page were taken on the Android build in an emulator, the TV ones on the Google TV emulator and the Windows ones on a Windows 11 PC, all with CC0 panoramas from [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com); the headset captures were taken on a Quest 3.

<a id="roadmap"></a>
## Roadmap

What is not done yet, the most likely first. Nothing here is a promise, and feedback on the [issue tracker](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) helps decide what comes first.

- **The stores**: Google Play (build 28 in review; the TV listing in review), the App Store (version 3.3.1 in review, then version 3.3.2 with build 28), the Meta Horizon Store with build 29, the LG Content Store (the submission is in preparation).
- **Computers**: the test of each function by hand on a Windows PC, sign in with your phone included; Spatial 2.5D with the webcam on Windows, in development for November 2026; then the 360° video renderer on Linux and macOS, a test on a real Mac and a real Linux PC; then automatic updates and a signed macOS app.
- **TVs**: build 29 on a real Android TV, its video controls and the sign in with your phone included; webOS 0.2.0 on a real LG TV, with its shares, Plex and live view, then the LG Content Store version; the Samsung test build on a real Samsung TV; then the 360° videos on the sphere, the sound of the cameras and their recordings (not before December 2026).
- **Phones and tablets**: the Tapo live view and the sign in of big screens on iPhone and iPad with the next iOS release; a time bar in the iOS 360° video player, previous and next in the 360° players of phones, audio track and subtitle menus in the iOS flat player.
- **Immersive media**: dual fisheye .dng, levelling of GoPro and DJI videos from their own motion data, spatial videos in 3D on the Quest if its decoders allow it, previous and next between spatial photos in the headset.
- **Sources**: swiping between the files of a share folder, Digest authentication for WebDAV, a QR code to bring the Plex token from the computer, IPv6 for Plex, H.265 Tapo recordings, channels on the TV home screen.
- **Drones**: a test of the import page with a real DJI Neo 2, then the sound recorded by the phone merged into its clip; the drone's live video relayed from the phone to the Meta Quest. Nothing that sends a command to a drone is planned before those tests.
- **Upstream**: small pull requests to Immich for the parts the maintainers want, starting with the 360° photo viewer.

The full list, with the device checks still pending for each feature, is in [Release status](../docs/immuch360/release-status.md#roadmap).
