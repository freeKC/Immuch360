# Immuch360

**Your media. Any screen. Every perspective.**

Flat, 360° and 3D photos and videos from your device, your NAS, your Immich server, your cameras and your drone. No account needed. Immuch360 is a free and open source app that reads your photos and videos where they are and plays each one in the viewer that fits it: flat, as a sphere you look around in, in 3D, or all around you in a headset. It runs on phones, tablets, TVs, Meta Quest headsets and computers.

**New: your flat photos, in 3D.** Pick a photo on your computer and get a 3D photo, a 3D scene to step into, or a 360° panorama that imagines the rest of the place around you. In a Meta Quest 3 or 3S, one press on "AI relief" and any flat photo stands out. The AI runs on your own computer and headset, and nothing is sent anywhere. Experimental, and not yet tried in a headset by the author (first test on 30 October). [See how](#turn-one-photo-into-3d-a-scene-a-panorama).

## Who it is for

- **Your photos are flat** and you want them in 3D, as a scene to lean into in a headset, or inside a 360° panorama, without a 3D camera: [turn one photo into 3D, a scene, a panorama](#turn-one-photo-into-3d-a-scene-a-panorama), new, made by AI on your own computer.
- **You shoot 360° or 3D** and want to look around in your shots, not see them flat, doubled or stretched: [360°, VR180 and 3D](#explore-panoramas-360-vr180-and-3d).
- **Your photos and films sit on a NAS, a computer, Plex or Immich** and you want them on the TV without copying anything: [on the TV](#watch-on-the-tv-with-the-remote), [NAS, shares and Plex](#play-the-files-of-a-nas-a-computer-a-media-server-or-plex).
- **You have a Meta Quest** and want to step inside your own photos and videos: [in the headset](#step-inside-your-photos-and-videos-with-a-meta-quest).
- **You run Immich** and want its app, plus the immersive viewers and the big screens you sign in from your phone: [your Immich library](#keep-your-immich-library-with-more), [compared with Immich](#compared-with-immich).
- **You watch your home with Tapo cameras** and want them next to your photos, on the TV too (not yet checked with a real camera): [home cameras](#watch-your-home-cameras-on-the-big-screen).
- **You fly a DJI drone** and want its live picture on your phone without DJI Fly, and in a Meta Quest or on an Android TV for the people with you (experimental): [your drone](#see-what-your-drone-sees-on-your-phone).

There is no Immuch360 account to create, and nothing goes to us: the app talks only to your devices and to the servers you choose, and downloads an AI component only when you ask for it.

Take the app from the store of your device where it is published, or from the [Releases](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases) page meanwhile.

### iPhone and iPad

<a id="android-tv-and-google-tv"></a>

## How to

<a id="turn-one-photo-into-3d-a-scene-a-panorama"></a>
### Turn one photo into 3D, a scene, a panorama

Most of your photos are flat, and you cannot go back and take them again in 3D. Immuch360 gives them depth. On your computer, one photo becomes a **3D photo**, a **3D scene** you lean into in a headset, or a **360° panorama** that imagines the rest of the place around you. A Quest 3 or 3S does the relief itself, for any flat photo. Everything runs on your own computer and headset: no cloud, no account, your originals never changed. New in build 32 and desktop build 13, experimental and on by default. The Immich app has nothing like it.

**On your computer**, with desktop build 13 on Windows, Linux or macOS:

1. Select one or more photos in the library or in a folder, then "Make 3D (AI)".
2. Pick what you want: "3D photo", "3D scene", "360° panorama", or "3D version of a 360° photo" for your own 360° shots. The box says what AI will estimate or invent ("About 96% of this panorama will be invented by AI"), about how long it takes on this computer, and where the file goes: "Next to the original" or "A folder of my choice". A type this computer cannot do stays greyed, with its reason, such as "Not yet on Mac" or "The graphics card has 6.0 GiB of memory, 360° panoramas need 7.5 GiB".
3. The first time, the box offers the components of that type: Download, once. They run on this computer and work offline afterwards.
4. Convert. The jobs queue on two lanes, the processor and the graphics card, with their steps and the time left; Cancel stops one at any moment, and quitting the app asks first.
5. Open the result. A 3D photo shows as "Left eye", "Side by side", "Wiggle" (the animation at the top of this page) or "Red and cyan" for glasses; a scene has "Preview in 3D", turned with the mouse or the touchpad, and "Export for other 3D apps (.glb)"; a panorama opens in the 360° viewer.

Every new file goes next to its original, never over it, or into a folder of your choice when the original's folder takes no new file; on a network folder, only once you say yes for that folder ("Immuch360 writes new files there, never the originals"). The library links each version to its original with its badge, "3D AI", "3D scene" or "360° AI", in the album "Immuch360 AI versions", which stays out of your Immich backup unless you turn on "Also back up AI versions"; careful, "Select all" in the backup albums picks this album too and turns that switch on. To see a file in the Quest, save it on your NAS, back it up to your Immich server or, on Windows, turn on "Share this computer".

**In a Meta Quest**, with build 32:

- **AI relief**: open any flat photo, from your library or a share, and press "AI relief" in the top bar: the headset estimates the relief itself and shows the photo in 3D, "Relief estimated by AI". The model, 99.5 MB, downloads the first time you press it, and the reliefs are kept in a cache. "Open AI reliefs in 2.5D (experimental)", in Settings, Advanced, "AI and 3D", makes near objects move in front of the background as you move your head. On a Quest 2 or a Quest Pro the button stays greyed, "Not tested on this headset yet", until you turn on "AI relief on Quest 2 and Quest Pro (untested)" there.
- **3D photos and scenes from the computer**: "View in 3D" shows a 3D photo in true 3D. "View the 3D scene" on the original, or "Explore the 3D scene" on the scene, puts the scene in your room: lean in, or glide with the left thumbstick inside the rebuilt area ("Room to move: about 0.5 m each way" for the room at the top of this page); the layers flatten and darken at its edge, a click on a thumbstick resets the view, and B or Y goes back.
- **Panoramas**: a generated panorama opens all around you, facing your photo, with "Generated by AI beyond the photo's edges" and a thin outline of the original photo, which fade after a few seconds; its 3D version is in true 3D.

**On phones, tablets and TVs**, the files a computer made open as they are, and nothing is generated there. A panorama opens in the 360° viewer with the "360° AI" badge, the sentence and the outline on Android phones, tablets and Android TV; on iPhone, iPad, LG and Samsung TVs it opens as a plain 360° photo for now. A scene shows as its photo, with a small 3D SCENE mark in its corner; on Android, its details add "Open it on a Meta Quest to explore it". A 3D photo shows its two eyes side by side.

**What is real and what is not, always said.** It is the point, not the small print. The relief of a 3D photo is estimated from one photo, and what the photo hides is filled in and is not real. A scene is "Reconstructed from one photo, explore within its limits". A panorama keeps your real pixels where they were and invents the rest, about 90% of the sphere for a typical photo, and the box says how much before you start. The app says it where you look, with "Relief estimated by AI", "Generated by AI beyond the photo's edges" and the badges, and the files say it too: in our XMP, and for the scenes and the panoramas in the IPTC digital source type of an image made with AI, so that other apps can tell a generated panorama from a real 360° photo. Your originals are never changed, and nothing leaves your computer or your headset. As the box puts it: "A finished conversion does not guarantee a good result."

**What was checked**, by 29 October 2026, with test builds of this code: 3D photos by the Linux app in a container on a Linux PC, offline too; scenes by the Windows app on a laptop with an RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (ten in 2 min 39 s) and by the engine on the Linux PC; on that laptop, four panoramas in 4 min 16 s to 4 min 44 s each, their 3D versions in about 30 s, with Cancel, a killed app, another program holding the graphics card, and no network connection of the app during the tests.

Limits: no 360° panorama on a Mac yet ("Not yet on Mac"), nor without a suitable graphics card, and the box says why ("No graphics card with Vulkan found", "Integrated graphics share the computer's memory: too slow for panoramas", with "Try on integrated graphics (untested)" to try anyway); inside a panorama, the edge of your photo can show as a sharp step in tone; most scenes say "Field of view guessed: shapes may be off"; a scene needs about 2 GB of free memory ("Not enough memory" otherwise); with AI versions backed up, Immich's duplicate finder pairs each photo with its scene and often suggests keeping the scene, the heavier file: keep the original. Phones and TVs make nothing, and the headset makes reliefs only; a scene comes from one photo, not yet from several photos of the same place.

**The components**: on the computer, Settings, "AI and 3D" lists the components of each feature with their size, Download and Remove, then "Remove all AI components" and "Move the AI components" to another drive; "AI features" off hides every AI button and downloads nothing. Each file is checked by its SHA-256. On Windows they sit in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\com.aprogsys\Immuch360 Desktop\ai-components`, which the uninstaller deletes (not a folder you moved them to); on Linux in `~/.local/share/immuch360/ai-components` on most systems, and on a Mac in `~/Library/Application Support/com.aprogsys.immuch360.desktop/ai-components`. In the Quest, "AI and 3D" is in Settings, Advanced, with the relief cache.

More: the notes of [build 32](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/v3.3.0-rc.0-32) and [desktop build 13](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/releases/tag/desktop-v3.3.0-rc.0-13), and [the models, their licences, their sources and their sums](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/blob/immuch360/licenses/ai-components/MODEL-LICENSES.md). ### Watch your photos and videos, with or without a server

Immuch360 is first a gallery and a player for ordinary photos and videos, and it needs neither an account nor a server. "Use without a server" opens the app on the photos and videos of the device, with every viewer, the 360° list and the network sources, and nothing leaves the device. With an Immich server, the timeline, the albums, the favourites, the memories and the search come from it. Videos play in a native player with sound, seeking, a buffering indicator and a choice of audio tracks, in the 360°, Spatial and computer players and, from build 29, in the flat player of the Android app, which shows subtitles too.

<img src="../.github/readme/b19-library-no-server.png" width="260" alt="The Library tab without a server: the 360° entry at the top, then On this device with two 360° photos, and Network shares with a share named NAS">

1. Open the app. The welcome page shows "Your media. Any screen. Every perspective." and three choices: tap the first one, "Use without a server". A server can be connected later, from Settings or the Library tab.
2. Allow access to your photos and videos. If you refuse, the app says "The app cannot see the photos of this device", with "Allow access" and "Open the settings".
3. The Photos tab shows the timeline of the device; Search and Albums stay greyed out, as they come from the server. The Library tab has 360° at the top, then On this device (the albums of the device) and Network shares. The menu at the top right says "This device only": nothing is sent anywhere.

<a id="sign-in-a-tv-a-headset-or-a-computer-with-your-phone"></a>
### Sign in a TV, a headset or a computer with your phone

**Scan. Sign in. Watch.** Typing a server address, an email and a password with a TV remote, or in a headset, takes minutes. With Immuch360 on your phone, signed in to your Immich server, a TV, a Meta Quest or a computer is signed in within seconds: the big screen shows a QR code, the phone scans it, you confirm, and the screen opens on your library. With the phone app, your password stays on the phone. The Immich app has nothing like it.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/sign-in-with-your-phone.png" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: a TV shows a QR code on its sign-in page, and a phone held in front of it scans the same code">
</p>

1. On the big screen (Android TV and Google TV, the Meta Quest window, Windows, Linux or macOS), choose "Connect to an Immich server": the sign-in page shows "Sign in with your phone", a QR code and a 10 character code, beside the server form.
2. On the phone, signed in to your server: Settings, "Connect a TV or computer", then "Scan the code". In a Meta Quest, whose screen a phone cannot scan, choose "Enter a code instead" and type the code the headset shows.
3. The phone asks "Sign (the screen) in?" and shows a six digit number: check that the screen shows the same one, then tap Sign in. The screen opens on your timeline.

How it stays safe: the phone and the screen must be on the same home network, and the code lasts two minutes at most and works once. The phone asks your server for a new session for that screen, named after it ("Immuch360 on Living room TV"), and sends it to the screen encrypted, so your password never leaves the phone. "Connect a TV or computer" lists the screens this phone signed in, each with Sign out, and Immich lists them in Account settings, Authorized devices, where you can revoke them too. Signing the phone out signs them out as well.

The phone side runs on Android now, and on iPhone and iPad from the next iOS release. Without Immuch360 on the phone, scan the code with the camera of the phone: a page served by the screen opens in the browser, for your email and password, encrypted and used once. LG TVs sign in with a QR code of their own, in the browser of the phone; from build 28 the scanner of the phone app recognises it and opens that page. Samsung TVs are signed in by typing for now.

More: [sign in with your phone](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#sign-in-a-tv-a-headset-or-a-computer-with-your-phone), [what the screens send and keep](../docs/immuch360/technical-details.md#sign-in-with-your-phone).

### Play the files of a NAS, a computer, a media server or Plex

Your videos sit on a NAS, a computer, a media server or Plex. Immuch360 finds the servers of your network by itself, browses them and plays their photos and videos in place, in every viewer of the app, without copying anything: SMB (Samba, Windows shares), WebDAV (Nextcloud, Synology and others) and DLNA media servers (Jellyfin, Emby, minidlna, a NAS, a TV box). A phone can also share its own gallery on the Wi-Fi for a headset or any WebDAV client, and so can a Windows computer.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/b19-add-share.png" width="260" alt="The Add a share page: Name, Server name or address, Port (optional), Share, Choose a share, Start folder (optional), User name, Password, Test the connection, and the result Connected, 2 entries in the start folder">
  <img src="../.github/readme/b19-share-folder.jpg" width="260" alt="A folder of a network share as a grid of thumbnails: 360° photos with the 360° badge and a 360° video with a play mark, the Select button at the top right">
</p>

1. Open the Library tab, then Network shares, then + and "A network share (NAS, computer, media server)". From build 24, "Add a network share or a media server" on the welcome page opens Network shares directly, without a server.
2. Tap a server under "Found on the network", or fill the form by hand: type, server name or address, share or path, start folder, user name and password.
3. Tap "Test the connection", then Save. Tap the share to browse it: folders first, then the photos and videos, with the 360° badge on the files recognised as 360°.
4. For a Plex server: in Network shares, + and "A Plex Media Server", then tap your server under "Found on the network", paste a token once ("How to get it" on the page says where to find it), then "Test the connection" and Save.
5. With an Immich server connected, select files in a folder and tap "Upload to Immich" to send them to your server.

The players read the bytes they need through a bridge inside the app, with byte ranges, so seeking works and nothing is stored on the device; an SMB video is read over up to six connections in parallel for smooth playback. From build 29, films recorded as M2TS (Blu-ray, AVCHD camcorders, TV recorders) play in the Android app from SMB and WebDAV shares and from DLNA servers that list them as transport streams, and a DLNA server that lists a size of 0 no longer gives an empty video.

**Plex Media Server, without plex.tv**: the app pairs with your server directly, with a token you paste once, and reads the original files of your libraries live, so 360° stays 360°, at home and, through your port forwarding, away from home. On an LG TV, Plex is signed in with a code on plex.tv/link.

More: [network shares](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#network-shares-a-nas-a-computer-or-a-media-server), [share this phone on the network](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#share-this-phone-on-the-network), [DLNA media servers](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#dlna-media-servers), [Plex Media Server](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#plex-media-server-without-plextv).

<a id="your-dji-drones-live-video-on-your-phone-experimental"></a>
<a id="see-what-your-drone-sees-on-your-phone"></a>
#### See what your drone sees, on your phone

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/drone-live-phone.png" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: two hands hold a drone remote controller with a phone clipped on top, whose screen shows a live aerial view of a coast, while a small drone flies over the bay at dusk">
</p>

See what your DJI drone sees on your Android phone, without DJI Fly and without any account: plug a DJI RC-N3 remote controller into the phone with its cable, and Immuch360 shows the drone's live video full screen in landscape, H.265 in 1080p, with the flight values read from the cable. The remote keeps full control of the drone. It was tried with an RC-N3, a DJI Neo 2 and a Samsung Galaxy S24+: on the ground with build 29, and in flight with test builds of build 30 on 24 and 25 October, then with build 30 on 26 October.

**Which remotes and drones**, on 26 October 2026: the live video comes through a DJI remote that holds the phone on its cable. Tried another pair? On the live screen, open Advanced, then Diagnostics, and tell us in [Issues](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) which drone and remote you used and what Diagnostics shows.

- **Tested**: a DJI Neo 2 fitted with its Digital Transceiver, which it needs to fly with any remote, and a DJI RC-N3, with a Samsung Galaxy S24+; on the ground on 23 October and in flight, indoors, from 24 to 26 October.
- **Expected, not tried**: the RC-N3 with the other drones DJI lists for it (Neo, Flip, Mini 4 Pro, Mini 5 Pro, Air 3, Air 3S, Lito 1, Lito X1, and the Avata 360 by its own guide), and the RC-N2, which holds the phone on the same cable, with the same drones and the Neo 2. The app shows H.265 video only, and the flight values are proven on the Neo 2 only: with another drone the picture or the values may be missing.
- **Not supported**: remotes with their own screen (DJI RC, RC 2, RC Pro 2), and so the Mavic 4 Pro, which DJI ships with one of them; DJI goggles; the RC-N1, the RC-N1C and older remotes; a drone flown from the phone over Wi-Fi, without a remote.

It only listens. No command is ever sent to the remote or the drone: the one byte the page writes each second, from 20 seconds after the remote opens, only tells whether the remote takes data, and carries no command.

On the live screen: the drone's battery and the motor time and, in flight, the height above the take off point, the vertical speed, the speed and the distance below the drone. A value goes there only once our own flight captures prove it: the height, the speeds and the distance below were proven on the captures of two indoor flights on 25 October, up to 1.8 m high and 1.5 m/s, not beyond yet; showing them there is new in build 30 and first tested with it. The height shows about 1.5 s after lift off, once the drone has left the ground or the hand, and stays hidden until the motors stop if it ever jumps in a way no aircraft can. Advanced adds the battery's voltage and temperature, the heading (its zero not checked against north) and the motors, and Diagnostics every number the link gives, the battery current included. No satellites, position or distance from home are shown. The numbers are not compared with DJI Fly yet: do not fly by them.

**Obstacle ring**, on by default: when the drone's sensors see something close, a translucent circle shows around the middle of the picture, with an arc towards the obstacle, amber turning red as it gets closer, its distance beside it and a short tick that speeds up; with nothing close, nothing shows. The circle and the direction of the arc were checked in flight, indoors, on 25 October, on a test build where the direction was an option; shown by default, it is first tested with build 30. In Advanced you set the distances ("Glow from", 3.0 m, and "Strong glow from", 1.0 m), choose a frame at the edges of the picture instead of the circle, or turn off the ring, its sound or its vibration. It shows only what the drone's sensors report: nothing in Sport mode or with obstacle avoidance off, and no glow does not mean the way is clear. Information only: Immuch360 never steers or stops the drone.

1. The Drone card is in the Library, and "DJI live video" on its page: "DJI live video (experimental)", in Settings, Advanced, is on by default.
2. Turn on the drone and the remote, close DJI Fly, then open "DJI live video" and read the safety notice.
3. Plug the remote into the phone with its cable. If Android asks which app should open it, choose Immuch360 without "Always", which would take the remote away from DJI Fly; if it asks for a USB mode, choose charging only. Plugging in the remote then opens this screen by itself ("Open this screen when the remote is plugged in", on by default in Advanced).
4. Remote, Drone and Picture light up in turn, and the picture comes full screen within a few seconds. Three buttons (leave, "Send to a headset or a TV" and Advanced) and the values sit in the black bands beside the picture. The notes, such as "Live", fade after about five seconds; a tap anywhere brings them back, and nothing on the picture acts on the drone. An error that asks you to act stays.
5. Advanced has "Save a capture for a bug report", which writes the link's capture to `Download/Immuch360/dji-link`, "Play a saved capture", to play one again without the remote, and Diagnostics, with every number the link gives.

The drone's photos and videos cannot be reached through the remote, which does not open the drone's storage: copy them from the drone itself over USB or from its card, as [below](#bring-a-drones-or-a-camera-cards-photos-and-videos-to-immich). Keep the saved captures to yourself, as the page says: they hold the drone's video and the serial numbers of the remote and the aircraft, and may hold its position; a report in [Issues](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) with what Diagnostics shows helps, above all with other DJI remotes.

<a id="watch-it-in-a-meta-quest-or-on-an-android-tv"></a>
#### Watch it in a Meta Quest or on an Android TV

The phone plugged into the remote can send the drone's picture and values to Immuch360 in a Meta Quest or on an Android TV or Google TV on the same local network, for the people with you. A headset shows them in a window or, larger, in a flight view in front of you, and a TV full screen, with the same values and obstacle ring as the phone. They only watch: nothing a headset or a TV does reaches the remote or the drone. Their notes, such as "Watching only", fade after a few seconds and come back with a key, a tap or a button. The drone's picture reached a Quest 3 once, on 24 October, over a home Wi-Fi; the headset window and the flight view with the values and the obstacle ring are first tested with build 30, and the Quest 2, Pro and 3S have not been tried. Should the circle hide the middle of the picture in the headset, "Frame at the edges" in its menu keeps the middle clear. On a TCL Android TV, the pairing and the picture were tried on 25 October, with stutter and a green cast on the earlier relay; on 26 October, with build 30, after the switch to the steady stream, the TV kept showing the picture through a flight where the drone sent 18 Mbit/s, with 7 freezes, the longest 1.7 s; how the picture looked was not recorded. From build 31 the safety notice on the TV keeps the focus of the remote (checked by widget tests, not yet on that TV); in build 30 the view under it could take the focus, so the notice could be neither read nor accepted, and the only way out was Back, which leaves the page. Flying while you watch the headset or the TV needs an observer next to you who keeps the drone in sight (EU open category); without one, watch the drone.

1. On the phone, on the live screen, tap "Send to a headset or a TV" and turn it on.
2. **Meta Quest**: open Immuch360, Library, Drone, and pick the phone; type the code the phone shows, once for that phone. The picture opens in the window; "Open the flight view" makes it larger, and B or Y brings the window back.
3. **Android TV and Google TV**: open Immuch360, Library, Drone. Under "Let this TV watch", the TV shows a code and its QR code: on the phone, in the same panel, tap "Add a TV" and scan it, or type it. Then select the phone on the TV and press OK: the picture comes full screen; OK shows or hides the numbers, and Menu opens the options.
4. The phone and the screen must be on the same local network. Away from home, turn on the phone's hotspot, in the 5 GHz band if it offers it, and join it from the headset or the TV.
5. For a bug report, the headset's menu and the TV's Options have Diagnostics and "Save a reception record", which keeps what that screen received.

**Relay quality**, new in build 30, in Advanced on the phone: in flight the drone sends about 18 Mbit/s, and on 25 October a home Wi-Fi path from the phone through the router to the TV carried that full stream only a third of the flight time; the rest of the time the TV's picture stuttered, broke up and took a green cast. With Automatic, the default, a screen whose Wi-Fi cannot keep up gets a steady picture that the phone encodes again for it, up to 1080p at 30 pictures a second and at the rate its Wi-Fi carries, made to stay clean, with less detail, instead of breaking up; it goes back to the drone's own picture at the next landing or take off, once its Wi-Fi has carried the steady picture for 30 s without a stall; on 26 October that TV's Wi-Fi never did, and the TV stayed on the steady picture, at 15 pictures a second for 44 of its 59 seconds. Full stream always sends the drone's picture as it comes, Steady stream always the phone's. The steady picture first ran on a phone on 26 October, with build 30: a Galaxy S24+, encoding it in hardware HEVC at a constant bit rate. A TV on an Ethernet cable, or a screen joined to the phone's own hotspot, leaves one Wi-Fi hop instead of two.

Not in this build: the drone on iPhone and iPad (not started), the relay to LG and Samsung TVs, and the proof of the values above 1.8 m and 1.5 m/s, then the satellites and the distance from home, which wait for an outdoor flight.

<a id="your-dji-drones-photos-and-videos-on-your-immich-server"></a>
<a id="bring-a-drones-or-a-camera-cards-photos-and-videos-to-immich"></a>
#### Bring a drone's or a camera card's photos and videos to Immich

Import the photos and videos of a DJI drone over USB, straight to your Immich server: plug the drone or a card reader into an Android phone, pick the new files and send them, with the videos placed on the map from the drone's flight data and the drone's 360° spheres shown as 360°. The Immich app backs up only what is already in the phone's gallery, and the Immich server cannot read the place of a DJI Neo 2 video yet. This page is experimental, on Android phones and tablets only, and has not been tried with a real drone yet.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/dji-drone-import.png" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: a drone on a terrace table, plugged into a phone by a cable, and its photos and videos going in an arc from the drone to the gallery of the phone">
</p>

1. Open the Drone card in the Library, then "Photos and videos from the drone". Its setting, "DJI drones (experimental)" in Settings, Advanced, is on by default; turn it off there to hide that page.
2. Plug the drone (a Neo 2 can stay off) or a card reader into the phone with a USB-C data cable; a DJI remote without a screen (RC-N1, RC-N2, RC-N3) holds no photos. On the DJI drone page, tap "Choose the drone or the card", choose it in the side menu of the picker, open its DCIM folder or keep the whole drive, then "Use this folder" and Allow.
3. The page lists the photos and videos by day, without the .LRF, .SRT, .THM and .m4a side files. Tap "Select all new", then "Upload to Immich"; the files sent before carry a cloud mark and are skipped. "Keep on the device" copies the files to the phone's gallery instead, in the album "Immuch360 import".
4. For a video to land on the map, turn on Video Subtitles in DJI Fly (video mode, Settings, Camera) before you fly: the drone then writes a .SRT file next to each video, the app reads the place from it and sets it on your server after the upload, when Immich found none.

The 360° spheres of DJI drones (Mini, Air, Mavic and others, recognised from their camera) show as 360° in Immuch360 even without 360° tags. Immich needs those tags: the upload option "Tag drone spheres as 360° for Immich", off by default, sends the photo unchanged with a small .xmp file beside it, so that Immich shows it as a sphere too. The Neo 2 has no sphere or panorama mode: its photos and videos are flat. When the phone recorded the sound of a Neo 2 clip, that sound is in a separate .m4a file: the page marks the clip, and Immich receives it without sound (download it with DJI Fly to keep the sound). Any camera card with a DCIM folder works the same way in a card reader. Nothing is sent to DJI.

More: [DJI drones over USB](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#dji-drones-photos-and-videos-over-usb-experimental), [DJI link diagnostics](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#dji-link-diagnostics-experimental).

<a id="open-the-raw-files-of-360-cameras"></a>
### Open the raw files of 360° cameras

The raw .insp photos and .insv videos of Insta360 cameras (one or two lenses, in one track, two tracks or two files), the .360 files of the GoPro MAX and the .osv files of the DJI Osmo 360 are stitched by the app as they play, with the calibration written in the file, with nothing to install anywhere.

1. Put the raw files where the app reads them: an SMB or WebDAV share, your Immich server (it takes .insp and .insv only), or the folders of a computer. The gallery of an Android phone does not list raw files, so a raw file copied to the phone shows neither in its gallery nor in the app.
2. Keep the two files of a recording made of two files together: the app looks for the other one next to the first.
3. Open the file and tap 360°. A photo shows "Raw 360° file, stitched by the app" with the source of its calibration; a video is stitched as it plays.

Linux and macOS play raw videos flat, and dual fisheye .dng files show flat for now.

More: [raw 360° camera files](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#raw-360-camera-files-without-the-cameras-app).

<a id="turn-a-photo-with-one-key"></a>
### Turn a photo with one key

Rotate a photo 90° with one press, without opening an editor: a button, a key of the keyboard or a colour key of the TV remote, on every platform. The Immich app turns a photo in its editor; Immuch360 does it with one key, and for your own photos on an Immich server 2.6 or later it goes through that same server editor, so the web app and your other devices show them turned. Any other photo is turned on this device only. 360°, 180° and 3D photos never turn, so the immersive views stay level.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/rotate-one-key.png" width="720" alt="Illustration, not a capture: a phone shows a photo turning a quarter turn clockwise, above a row of round buttons with the rotate button lit">
</p>

1. **Phones, tablets and the Meta Quest window**: open the photo and tap Rotate in the bottom row, before Edit, to turn it clockwise; a long press turns it the other way. With a keyboard, R and Shift+R.
2. **Android TV and Google TV**: the yellow key of the remote turns the photo clockwise and the blue key anticlockwise, wherever the focus is in the viewer. On a remote without colour keys, press Down: the bar under the photo shows with Rotate selected (one Right away when the photo has a text button), and each OK turns the photo.
3. **LG TVs (webOS)**: yellow and blue as on Android TV, R and Shift+R on a keyboard, or the turn button of the photo's controls. Samsung TVs turn a photo with one key too.
4. **Windows, Linux and macOS**: R turns clockwise, Shift+R anticlockwise, or the rotate button in the top bar, before full screen.
5. **Photos of a network share, Plex or a camera**: the rotate button first in the top bar, R and Shift+R, or the yellow and blue keys.

Where the turn is saved: a photo of your own account on Immich 2.6 or later is turned through the server's photo editor, which keeps the original file and makes a turned picture (a thin bar shows while it works; four turns, or one turn back, return to the original). The photos of the device, of a share, of a partner or a shared album, and every photo on an older server are turned on this device only, for that photo, and the file is never changed; the app says so once for an Immich photo. In the Android, iOS and computer apps, a turn kept on the device shows in the viewer, not yet in the thumbnails, the slideshow and the memories. Videos, the locked folder and every 360°, 180° or 3D photo have no Rotate.

<a id="use-your-computer-as-the-media-player"></a>
### Use your computer as the media player

On a Windows computer, Immuch360 Desktop reads the folders you choose with no server at all, plays flat and 360° videos in a player of its own, browses the same shares and servers, and can share the computer with a headset at home. Linux and macOS have it as previews, with 360° videos played flat.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../.github/readme/desktop-b4-video.jpg" width="440" alt="The video player of Immuch360 Desktop on Windows: a video of a street playing in the window, with the pause button, the time bar, Share and Upload to Immich">
</p>

1. Install the preview, or unzip it and start `immuch360.exe`, see [Install](#windows). On the welcome page, "Use without a server" opens the choice of your folders, and "Connect to an Immich server" the login page, with its QR code for your phone.
2. A photo, a video or a folder opened with the program, or dropped on its window, opens at once, a 360° one in its 360° view; several files open together on one page. Photos and videos get it in "Open with"; it never makes itself their default program.
3. In a viewer, the keyboard drives it: Space plays and pauses, J and L go back and forward, F is full screen, and R and Shift+R turn a photo.

More: [on a Windows computer](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#on-a-windows-computer-immuch360-desktop-preview), [platform support](../docs/immuch360/platform-support.md).

Immuch360 keeps the whole Immich mobile app and adds what is around it. In short:

| | Immuch360, compared with the Immich app |

| **Tapo cameras** | the live view and the recordings of the memory card, next to your photos; not yet checked with a real camera. |

| **Videos** | audio and subtitle menus in the Android player, which the Immich app's Android player does not have; the original file or the transcoded stream, chosen per video; the technical details of a video. |
| **Your drone** | a DJI drone's photos and videos sent over USB, with the videos placed on the map from its flight data, where the Immich app backs up only what is already in the phone's gallery; and the drone's live video through an RC-N3 remote, with its flight values and an obstacle ring, on the phone and relayed to a Meta Quest or an Android TV. Both experimental. |
| **Where to report** | [Immuch360 Issues](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues), not the Immich project. |

<a id="privacy"></a>
## Privacy

Nothing goes to the developer: no account, no analytics, no crash reporting service, no advertising. The app talks only to the servers you choose (your Immich server, your shares, your Plex server, your cameras) and, without a server, to nothing at all. Passwords and tokens stay in the secure storage of the device; LG and Samsung TVs have none for the app, so there they stay in the app's own storage, not encrypted. The camera is used on the device only, by the Spatial 2.5D player and to scan the code of a screen you sign in; its images are never stored and never sent anywhere. A screen signed in from your phone gets a session of its own, sent encrypted on your home network, never your password; an LG TV is signed in by a page in the phone's browser, which sends your email and password to the TV unencrypted on your home network, and the TV keeps only the session. The captures and reception records of the drone pages stay on the device unless you share them: they hold the drone's video and may hold its position, and a capture the serial numbers of the remote and the aircraft. The drone's picture and values go from the phone only to the headsets and TVs you pair, on your local network, not encrypted, as with a phone shared on the Wi-Fi. Nothing is sent to DJI, nor to TP-Link. The full policy is in [PRIVACY.md](../PRIVACY.md), and the details per source in the [user guide](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md#privacy).

**The AI features** work on your devices only. The photo, its relief, its scene and its panorama are computed on your computer or in your headset and sent nowhere. The app connects only to download a component you asked for, from the releases of this project on GitHub or, failing that, from the model's official page when it has one, checks each file by its SHA-256, and then works offline. ONNX Runtime, which runs the models on a computer, sends telemetry to Microsoft in its official builds; Immuch360 turns it off. The new files go next to your originals, never over them, and reach your Immich server only if you turn on "Also back up AI versions" ("Select all" in the backup albums turns it on too).

What is not done yet, the most likely first. Nothing here is a promise, and feedback on the [issue tracker](https://github.com/freeKC/Immuch360/issues) helps decide what comes first.

### Advanced features

What the app can read and play, for the reader who wants the whole extent. Each line is detailed in the [user guide](../docs/immuch360/user-guide.md) and the [compatibility page](../docs/immuch360/compatibility.md).

<details>
<summary><b>Formats, sources, playback and hardware, in detail</b></summary>

- **Formats**: flat photos and videos (what the device decodes: MP4, MOV, MKV and more); equirectangular 360° photos and videos; partial panoramas (GPano crop); VR180; stereoscopic 3D, top and bottom or side by side, read from the file (the st3d box) or guessed from the frame; Apple spatial photos (HEIC stereo pairs) and spatial videos (MV-HEVC, one eye); raw Insta360 .insp and .insv, GoPro .360, DJI .osv; 10 bit HLG and PQ sources tone mapped on the sphere.
- **Projection and metadata**: GPano and spherical metadata read from the file, with range requests on a server or a share; the initial view of the camera; the spheres of DJI drones recognised from their camera, with an optional .xmp sidecar so that Immich flags them too; "View as 360°" remembered on the device; VR180 recognised from the file or its name; the place of a drone video read from its .SRT flight log; metadata kept intact because the original file is played, never a converted copy; a quarter turn saved through the Immich editor or kept on the device, never on an immersive file.
- **Native playback**: Media3 on Android and the TV, SceneKit and AVFoundation on iOS, the Meta Spatial SDK in the headset, libmpv on Windows; the original or the transcoded stream chosen from what the device decodes; a list of the device's video decoders in Settings, Advanced; the audio track choice, subtitles in the Android player and a buffering indicator.
- **Raw files stitched on the device**: a Flutter fragment shader for photos; a GPU effect in the player for single track videos; two hardware decoders at once and a GPU compositor for videos with one lens per track or per file (OpenGL ES 3 on Android and the Quest, Metal on iOS, and on Windows, from desktop build 5, two libmpv players whose frames the app pairs on the graphics chip); the camera's own calibration (Mei, V6 and equidistant models) and accelerometer record; fallbacks to one lens, the transcoded stream, the camera's LRV copy or the unstitched video when the device cannot run two decoders.
- **Sources**: the device's gallery; the folders of a computer; a drone or a camera card over USB, through the system folder picker (Android, experimental); a DJI RC-N3 remote controller over its cable for the drone's live video (Android, experimental), relayed on the local network to a Meta Quest or an Android TV; an Immich server; SMB 2 and 3, WebDAV, DLNA/UPnP; Plex over HTTPS checked against the server's own certificate; Tapo cameras over the local network (HTTPS and RTSP); a phone or a computer shared on the Wi-Fi as a WebDAV server; servers found by Bonjour/mDNS, a local network scan, SSDP, GDM and the TP-Link discovery, and followed when they change address.
- **On demand reading**: a local bridge serves the players with byte ranges and a per session token; large blocks, read ahead and up to six SMB connections in parallel; thumbnails from a frame of each video; nothing copied to the device.
- **Hardware**: hardware decoders used wherever they exist, with their measured limits (the Quest 3 H.264 ceiling, two 2048x2048 lenses at most for software decoders); on Windows the 360° view drawn by the graphics chip from each decoded frame, the frame size measured on each computer, the integrated or dedicated chip detected.
- **Platform specifics**: the gyroscope and the Spatial 2.5D player on phones; the immersive view, controllers and hands on the Quest; the remote layout, the keyboard dialog and the TV banner on Android TV; on the computer, mouse and keyboard, full screen, a single window, files and folders opened with the program or dropped on its window, read like the files of your folders, HEIC, AVIF, TIFF and camera raw photos of the folders when Windows has a decoder for them, and the Windows data protection for passwords and tokens.
- **AI creator**: 3D photos as full width side by side JPEGs, left eye first, the relief from Depth Anything V2 Small through ONNX Runtime 1.31.0 on the processor (1.23.2 on an Intel Mac); scenes as a JPEG that carries a glTF scene in layers cut where the depth breaks, from MoGe-2 ViT-S and LaMa, with a .glb export; 360° panoramas of 6144 x 3072, generated by FLUX.2 klein base 4B and a 360° LoRA through stable-diffusion.cpp on Vulkan, the seam joined by MI-GAN, the detail raised by Real-ESRGAN and the real pixels put back; 3D versions of 360° photos, top and bottom; in every file our XMP (kind, origin, the SHA-1 of the original, quality flags), and in the scenes and the panoramas the IPTC digital source type; the relief of the Quest by LiteRT, on its graphics chip or its processor, kept in a cache; on the computer, a queue on two lanes and a gate that reads the graphics card through Vulkan and waits while other programs hold its memory; each component checked by its SHA-256, the Windows generator signed.
- **Privacy by construction**: no account, no cloud, no analytics; passwords and tokens in the device's secure storage (on LG and Samsung TVs, which have none for the app, in the app's own storage, not encrypted); a screen signed in from a phone gets a session of its own, sent encrypted on the home network, never the password (an LG TV gets the password from a page in the phone's browser, unencrypted on the home network, and keeps only the session); the camera of the Spatial player and of the code scanner used on the device only; shares, Plex and cameras reached on your network only; the AI computed on the device, its components downloaded only when you ask and checked by their SHA-256, the telemetry of ONNX Runtime turned off.

</details>
